# Trust Stewardship and the Non-Capture Principle

> Public explanatory summary. This page does not replace the TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust instrument, Foundation governing documents, applicable law, or legal advice. If this summary conflicts with an executed legal instrument, the executed instrument controls.

## Built for stewardship, not acquisition

TitleChain Foundation is designed to steward public-interest digital infrastructure across generations rather than to become an asset that can be bought by a sponsor, vendor, platform, government, founder, or future operator.

The constitutional layer is anchored through the **TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust**, a Wyoming purpose-trust structure whose governing framework provides for a maximum duration of one thousand years and describes the Trust as irrevocable.

The Trust's stated purpose is to preserve a human-first constitutional digital framework grounded in:

- human authority and self-ownership;
- privacy, meaningful consent, and data minimization;
- freedom from digital servitude and coercive surveillance;
- portability, interoperability, and the right to exit;
- accountable delegation to software and AI agents; and
- resistance to economic, technical, institutional, or governmental capture.

The one-thousand-year horizon is not a promise that any particular technology, company, protocol, blockchain, implementation, or operator will exist for one thousand years. It is a continuity design: the protected purpose is intended to outlive today's organizations, vendors, infrastructure, and founders.

## What the Trust layer does — and does not do

Under the Trust framework, Trust Property is limited to the constitutional artifacts, schemas, registries, cryptographic anchors, and governance descriptions identified by the governing instrument and its schedules.

The Trust is structurally separated from commercial operations. It is not intended to hold customer assets, operate a business, own customer relationships, or receive transactional revenue merely because an implementation uses the standards.

That separation matters. It prevents the constitutional layer from becoming dependent on the commercial success, financing structure, acquisition, insolvency, or strategic direction of any single implementation.

## The public architecture

The public model separates authority into layers that should not collapse into one another:

1. **TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust — constitutional stewardship.** Protects the enduring purpose and designated constitutional artifacts under its governing instrument.
2. **TitleChain Foundation — standards stewardship.** Maintains the public-interest program and institutional responsibilities consistent with that purpose.
3. **ICSN — open standards process.** Develops RFCs, schemas, conformance criteria, implementation guidance, and public review through documented governance.
4. **CYRUS — purpose-bound commons framework.** Provides a public-review licensing and purpose covenant for materials expressly designated under it. CYRUS does not by itself transfer patent, trademark, service, production-system, or Trust rights.
5. **Implementers — independent systems and services.** M5 may implement and test the standards, as may other independent implementations. No implementation defines the standard merely through adoption or funding.

This separation is part of the anti-capture architecture.

## The Non-Capture Principle

The governing framework rejects arrangements that concentrate rulemaking authority, operational control, and economic benefit in the same actor in a way that defeats the protected purpose.

Capture can arise through more than ownership. It can also arise through conditional funding, debt, exclusive licensing, preferential access, opaque technical dependencies, custody concentration, vendor lock-in, coercive surveillance, or informal control over standards and governance.

The principle therefore applies to everyone, including:

- founders and early contributors;
- donors and sponsors;
- companies and financial institutions;
- governments and public agencies;
- foundations, DAOs, cooperatives, and nonprofit entities;
- validators, node operators, infrastructure providers, and vendors; and
- future Foundation leadership.

No sponsor or contributor receives ownership of a person, identity, credential, standard, vote, Trust asset, or governance right merely because they provide funding, code, infrastructure, services, or adoption.

## What funding can never buy

Financial support may fund public work, including specifications, security review, documentation, conformance materials, reference implementations, open tooling, public infrastructure, education, and contributor coordination.

Funding does **not** by itself purchase:

- standards authority or a governance vote;
- the ability to amend the protected purpose;
- preferential conformance or certification treatment;
- exclusive implementation rights;
- access to private participant or member data;
- ownership of identity, credentials, wallets, vaults, or personal records;
- rights in the Trust or its protected property; or
- guaranteed adoption of a sponsor's product, platform, cloud, model, wallet, or service.

See [SPONSORS.md](SPONSORS.md) for the public funding policy and [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) for the standards decision process.

## Human authority remains the root

ICSN work is human-rooted. Software and AI agents may receive bounded authority, but consequential authority must remain attributable to an accountable human or lawful principal and remain subject to disclosed scope, revocation, and review.

A platform account, AI agent, wallet, validator, credential, or sponsor relationship does not create inherent authority over a person.

## No forced surveillance and no forced lock-in

The constitutional direction is that participation should not require unnecessary surveillance, profiling, data resale, behavioral extraction, or surrender of portable identity and records.

Implementations should support data minimization, selective disclosure, secure recovery, portability, cryptographic agility, provider substitution, and meaningful exit.

A system should not call itself sovereign merely because it uses decentralized technology. Sovereignty must be testable in the actual authority, data, dependency, recovery, and exit model.

## Continuity beyond any one implementation

The standards are intended to remain usable across changes in:

- software vendors and cloud providers;
- AI models and agent frameworks;
- wallet, custody, and ledger providers;
- cryptographic primitives;
- networks and public-notary mechanisms; and
- organizational leadership.

M5 is one activation and implementation environment. It is not required for participation in ICSN standards and does not receive standards authority merely by being an early implementation.

## Public infrastructure as proof of the mission

The Foundation's mission is not complete if sovereignty exists only as a document. Public infrastructure should make the principles usable.

The first activation layer is a human-controlled identity origin: an IAM pathway that can establish provenance and access without turning the sponsor, employer, platform, or application into the owner of the person.

See [PUBLIC-INFRASTRUCTURE-COMMITMENT.md](PUBLIC-INFRASTRUCTURE-COMMITMENT.md) for the no-charge foundational IAM commitment and the separation between GitHub participation, public infrastructure, and optional M5 services.

## Accountability

The Foundation intends to make the non-capture architecture inspectable through public governance records, RFC history, licensing designations, sponsor-impact reporting, conformance evidence, and machine-readable policy manifests where useful.

The companion [`constitutional/mission-lock.yaml`](constitutional/mission-lock.yaml) is an informational, machine-readable statement of the public policy invariants. It is not a substitute for law or the Trust instrument.

**Fund the commons. Never own the commons.**
