# Build the Public Economic Data Commons

> **Status: Public participation framework.** This page invites proposals, evidence, and collaboration. It is not an adopted standard, operating index, official statistic, regulated benchmark, procurement request, job posting, certification program, or representation that a contributor, company, product, dataset, or methodology is endorsed.

TitleChain Foundation invites companies, public agencies, researchers, standards bodies, educators, community organizations, independent experts, and builders to describe how they could strengthen interoperable public infrastructure for a digital-asset edge economy.

The goal is not to place every service inside one proprietary platform. It is to define open interfaces, evidence requirements, and governance boundaries that let independently operated products and providers contribute without capturing the commons.

## Choose a contribution track

| Track | What participants can contribute | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infrastructure and interoperability providers | Identity, credentials, storage, compute, networking, wallets, payments, ledgers, APIs, security, privacy, zero-knowledge, and replaceable adapters | Interface documentation, standards mappings, threat models, test results, portability plans, and deployment evidence |
| Economic data and index methodology | Data sources, statistical methods, classifications, sampling, aggregation, price discovery, revisions, uncertainty, and community or productive-value measures | Data dictionaries, provenance, licenses, methodology, coverage and bias analysis, reproducible examples, and revision policies |
| Products and public-interest use cases | Applications for people, businesses, institutions, communities, and public agencies that can use or test open interfaces | User need, affected communities, architecture mapping, accessibility review, measurable outcomes, and pilot limitations |
| Workforce and capability pathways | Public-safe summaries of expertise, contribution interests, changing roles, and skills people want to demonstrate | Optional portfolio links, self-declared skills, evidence-backed work, desired contribution, and holder-selected capability evidence |
| Training and curriculum | Courses, mentoring, assessment, simulations, apprenticeships, and role-transition pathways | Learning outcomes, prerequisites, accessibility, instructor qualifications, assessment method, and credential issuer |
| Independent assurance and governance | Legal, security, privacy, accessibility, conformance, audit, public-interest, and anti-capture review | Review criteria, conflicts of interest, test plans, findings, proposed safeguards, and accountable reviewers |

## Organize by the 37 SOPHIA tracks

The six contribution tracks above describe **how** someone can contribute. The [SOPHIA 37-Track Participation Map](docs/SOPHIA-37-TRACK-PARTICIPATION-MAP.md) describes **where** expertise, learning, products, and evidence may fit. It preserves all 37 canonical track names and organizes them into eight public navigation families:

1. Markets, finance, and assets
2. Data, AI, and software
3. Infrastructure, hardware, and security
4. Identity, health, workforce, and learning
5. Governance, legal, and public sector
6. Trade, cooperatives, and supply networks
7. Communications and adoption
8. M5 activation and operations

Participants can select one contribution mode and one SOPHIA track without being limited to either. Cross-disciplinary proposals may reference multiple tracks, and people remain free to propose missing or emerging roles.

## Where a provider can fit in the stack

The [L0-L8 + ZK reference architecture](architecture/M5-L0-L8-ZK-REFERENCE-ARCHITECTURE.md) is a dependency map, not a required vendor stack. Providers may propose one bounded component, an adapter between layers, a test harness, or an independently operated implementation.

| Layer | Public-review opportunities |
| --- | --- |
| L0 Constitution | Governance rules, change control, public-interest protections, and anti-capture mechanisms |
| L1 Identity | Human, organization, agent, and asset identifiers; credentials; resolution; recovery; and revocation |
| L2 Data Sovereignty | Holder-controlled stores, consent, data portability, privacy, provenance, and secure computation |
| L3 M5Wallet | Wallet and key interfaces, recovery, transaction intent, and human-authorized signing |
| L4 CA Engine | Authority, role, jurisdiction, policy, approval, restriction, and revocation evaluation |
| L5 Economics | Economic evidence, classifications, accounting, settlement, public-indicator interfaces, and methodology |
| L6 Sovereign AI | Bounded agents, model and tool adapters, evaluation, receipts, oversight, and fail-closed controls |
| L7 Node Network | Compute, storage, networking, edge devices, resilient operations, and replaceable infrastructure |
| L8 Product Suite | Accessible applications, business workflows, institutional tools, and public-interest services |
| ZK | Selective disclosure and proofs that reduce data exposure without removing accountability |

A provider does not need to support the entire architecture. Proposals should identify the smallest useful interface, the authority boundary, what remains independently operated, and how another conforming provider could be substituted.

## Public economic-data and index safeguards

Proposals for indicators, indexes, statistics, or data feeds should address:

- **purpose and users:** the question the measure is intended to answer and who could be affected;
- **provenance:** source, collection method, timestamps, transformations, and revision history;
- **rights and licensing:** the right to contribute, process, reproduce, and redistribute the data or methodology;
- **privacy and consent:** aggregation, minimization, disclosure risk, holder rights, and prohibited uses;
- **methodology:** definitions, classifications, sampling, weighting, calculations, missing data, and reproducibility;
- **quality and uncertainty:** coverage, bias, error, confidence, limitations, comparability, and versioning;
- **governance:** accountable maintainers, conflicts of interest, change control, correction, appeal, and anti-manipulation controls;
- **interoperability:** machine-readable schemas, identifiers, APIs, export, and provider substitution; and
- **status:** whether the contribution is research, a test dataset, a proposed method, or an independently operated service.

No dataset or methodology becomes an official statistic, regulated benchmark, or Foundation-approved index merely because it is discussed, linked, tested, or published in this repository. Commercial contributors must disclose relevant interests. Payment, sponsorship, market position, or data access must not purchase standards control or preferential methodological treatment.

## Company or provider proposal

Start a public Discussion with information that is safe and authorized to disclose:

1. Organization and public contact.
2. Product, service, dataset, methodology, or capability.
3. Contribution track and L0-L8/ZK layer or interface.
4. Public problem and affected users.
5. Proposed open interface, artifact, test, or pilot.
6. Current maturity: concept, prototype, deployed service, or independently verified implementation.
7. Standards, licenses, data rights, security controls, and portability or provider-substitution approach.
8. Evidence available for public review.
9. Privacy, legal, accessibility, market-integrity, and governance risks.
10. Commercial interests, dependencies, requested relationship, and what would remain proprietary.

Describe compatibility precisely. Participation does not imply partnership, procurement, certification, endorsement, investment, listing, or permission to use Foundation marks.

## Expertise, M5-CV, and changing roles

People can participate without publishing a conventional CV or activating an M5 account. A public introduction may include:

1. A short professional or lived-experience summary.
2. Skills or domain expertise the person chooses to disclose.
3. The contribution track, layer, or problem they want to help with.
4. Optional public portfolio, publication, code, credential, or work-sample links.
5. Skills they want to learn, test, teach, or have reviewed.
6. How they expect their role or industry to change and which safeguards or training would help.
7. The smallest useful contribution they can make now.

Do not post a private CV, home address, identity document, member record, nonpublic employment information, protected characteristics, wallet information, or confidential evidence. The optional M5-CV model is intended to support holder-selected capability evidence and transparent matching; it is not a public resume database or an automated human-worth score. Self-declared, evidence-backed, credential-backed, and independently verified claims must remain distinguishable.

Training interest, a suggested match, or a public contribution does not create employment, compensation, appointment, a credential, professional authority, agent delegation, or admission to a program. Any funded role, credential, or training place requires a separate, explicit process.

## How a proposal advances

1. **Introduce the contribution.** Use [Discussion 22 — Providers, Economic Data, Workforce & Training](https://github.com/orgs/TitleChain-Foundation/discussions/22) for an introduction or short proposal. For a substantial proposal, open a separate post in the [Public Infrastructure & Activation category](https://github.com/orgs/TitleChain-Foundation/discussions/categories/public-infrastructure-activation) and link it from Discussion 22.
2. **Route it transparently.** A maintainer identifies the relevant track, architecture layer, artifact, and review needs.
3. **Scope reviewable work.** If the idea is actionable, open or link an Issue with a defined output, responsible steward, evidence requirements, and status.
4. **Contribute an artifact.** Submit methodology, schema, mapping, test, documentation, reference code, curriculum, or other material under an explicit license.
5. **Test and review.** Record interoperability, privacy, security, accessibility, governance, and domain findings in public where safe.
6. **Record disposition.** Link accepted revisions, deferred questions, objections, independent implementations, and reasons. Publication alone is not adoption.

Security vulnerabilities and confidential implementation details must follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not the public intake. All participation follows [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), and the applicable [license designations](LICENSES/LICENSE-MATRIX.md).
