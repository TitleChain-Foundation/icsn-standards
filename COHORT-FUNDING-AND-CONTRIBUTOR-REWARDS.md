# Cohort, Funding and Contributor Rewards Framework

This document describes a proposed public framework for connecting sustained TitleChain Foundation participation with separate M5 cohort opportunities, grants and compensated work. It is an operating-policy draft, not a promise of admission, funding, employment, tokens or legal status.

The framework preserves four distinct rails:

1. **Open participation** — anyone may review and contribute under repository policies.
2. **M5 cohort eligibility** — qualifying contributors may be invited to apply through a separate, consent-based M5Bank onboarding process.
3. **Mission grants** — funds may support research, education, access, testing or other approved public-benefit work without purchasing standards authority.
4. **Compensated work** — defined services or deliverables may be paid under an approved employment, contractor, bounty or vendor arrangement.

No rail automatically activates another.

## Contributor-to-cohort pathway

Participants who make sustained, constructive commitments to the Foundation may become **eligible to apply** for a contributor place in an announced M5Bank cohort series. Eligibility may consider:

- a documented contribution record;
- alignment with the Code of Conduct and human-rooted principles;
- a defined skill, role, testing need or implementation responsibility;
- completion of applicable security and conflict-of-interest review; and
- cohort capacity, geography, legal eligibility and program priorities.

Admission remains separate from Foundation or GitHub membership. Each applicant must knowingly enter the M5 onboarding process and accept the applicable privacy, identity, account and service terms. A GitHub identity or public contribution must not be silently converted into an M5Bank account.

A contributor cohort may provide access to approved onboarding, training, test environments, implementation tools or marketplace pilots. Participation does not guarantee employment, funding, token allocation, investment return, governance power or continued access.

## Grant funding

The Foundation may seek donations, philanthropic grants, program-related funding and sponsorship for open standards, security review, conformance work, accessibility, education, contributor onboarding and public-interest development supporting the M5OpenAPI marketplace and related infrastructure.

Every grant program should publish or record:

- its charitable or public-benefit purpose;
- eligible applicant class and objective selection criteria;
- permitted and prohibited uses;
- award amount, currency or asset and payment schedule;
- milestones, reporting and acceptance requirements;
- reviewer identities or roles and conflict recusals;
- intellectual-property and open-publication requirements;
- tax, sanctions and jurisdictional checks; and
- suspension, recovery and appeal procedures where appropriate.

A **grant** supports an approved purpose and is not a disguised payment for services. When the Foundation controls defined work or purchases a deliverable, the arrangement should be treated and documented as compensation or procurement instead.

Grant selection must not be conditioned on a donation, sponsorship payment, token purchase, favorable standards vote or suppression of good-faith technical findings. Where applicable, individual-grant programs should use an objective, nondiscriminatory process and an eligible class related to the program purpose.

## Compensation for roles, skills and testing

Approved contributors may be paid for defined work such as:

- specification editing and technical research;
- software, schemas, SDKs and M5OpenAPI integrations;
- security, privacy, legal and economic review;
- test vectors, conformance testing, quality assurance and accessibility testing;
- documentation, translation, design, education and community operations; and
- accountable administration or other approved Foundation services.

Before work begins, a written award or agreement should identify the accountable payer, worker classification, scope, milestones, acceptance authority, rate or fixed amount, payment asset, valuation source and timestamp, tax reporting, intellectual-property terms, confidentiality boundaries, conflicts and dispute process.

Compensation may be denominated and paid in fiat currency, an approved payment stablecoin or another legally reviewed digital asset. The choice of payment rail does not change the nature of compensation. Each payment must be recorded at fair market value in the Foundation's functional currency when paid, with the required payroll, contractor, information-reporting, withholding, sanctions and accounting controls.

No token should be promised or distributed for contributor work until counsel has classified the asset and the transaction, including securities, commodities, money-transmission, tax, sanctions, consumer-protection and cross-border consequences. Transfer restrictions, custody, vesting and liquidity claims must be stated accurately. Wallet activation is a payment capability—not proof of identity, membership or governance authority.

## Proposed DUNA governance layer

The Foundation is evaluating activation under a decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association structure. It must not describe itself as an operating DUNA until the governing principles, member consent, membership threshold, jurisdictional requirements and other formation steps have been completed and recorded.

If activated, the governing principles should define:

- how a person knowingly becomes, remains or ceases to be a legal member;
- the relationship among human identity, member credentials, wallets and voting rights;
- proposal, quorum, voting, delegation, recusal and emergency processes;
- administrators and the limits of their authority;
- treasury custody, budgets, payment approvals and public reporting;
- reasonable compensation, expenses and member benefits consistent with the nonprofit purpose;
- conflicts, related-party transactions and independent review;
- records, privacy, audit, dispute and appeal processes; and
- amendment, continuity, dissolution and asset-disposition rules.

GitHub organization membership, an M5Bank account, a wallet, a credential or ownership of a token must not by itself establish DUNA membership unless the adopted governing principles and required consent expressly provide otherwise. Economic rewards must not be confused with distributions of Foundation profits.

## M5OpenAPI marketplace boundary

Foundation funding may accelerate open specifications, public APIs, conformance tooling, security work and other shared infrastructure that enables an interoperable M5OpenAPI marketplace. Commercial marketplace operation, customer accounts, transaction execution and proprietary services must remain in the authorized M5 operating layer unless the Foundation formally approves a different structure.

For every funded marketplace project, the award record should identify:

- the public-good output owned or stewarded by the Foundation;
- any proprietary implementation owned by an M5 operator or another party;
- the license for code, documentation, data and specifications;
- the party responsible for users, funds, custody, compliance and support; and
- the evidence required before a milestone is accepted and paid.

Standards decisions remain governed by technical merit and the public process. Funding, cohort status, compensation, account status or token ownership does not purchase a merge, vote, certification or standards outcome.

## Required approvals before launch

Before announcing funded cohort places or token-denominated rewards, the responsible entities should adopt:

1. governing principles and a legal-entity responsibility map;
2. conflict-of-interest, compensation and grantmaking policies;
3. worker-classification, tax, sanctions and payment procedures;
4. a digital-asset classification and approval process;
5. treasury, wallet, key-management and multi-party approval controls;
6. privacy and consent terms connecting Foundation participation to optional M5 onboarding;
7. standard grant, contractor, bounty and acceptance templates; and
8. public reporting that protects personal and production information.

This framework requires review by qualified legal, tax and accounting advisers in every jurisdiction where people are admitted or paid.
