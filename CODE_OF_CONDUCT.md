# M5Bank & TitleChain Foundation Code of Conduct for Sovereign Participation

## 1. Our commitment

M5Bank, TitleChain Foundation, and the M5 ecosystem exist to advance a world in which people, communities, and institutions can participate in economic, digital, and civic life with dignity, agency, security, and meaningful control.

We build toward a human-centered Internet 3.0: one in which identity, assets, data, knowledge, and participation are not merely extracted, surveilled, speculated upon, or controlled by distant intermediaries. We believe technology must expand the ability of people to govern their own lives, protect their communities, preserve their lawful rights, and create durable prosperity.

This Code of Conduct establishes the standards for how we work together. It applies to every participant in M5-related spaces, including:

- M5Bank members and customers.
- TitleChain Foundation trustees, stewards, staff, advisors, volunteers, and partners.
- Developers, researchers, auditors, node operators, validators, credential issuers, and service providers.
- Contributors to software repositories, documentation, standards, public forums, events, social channels, governance forums, and member communities.
- AI systems, agents, and automated services deployed in M5 environments, through the humans and entities responsible for them.
- Institutional partners, funders, licensors, community organizations, and public-sector participants.

Participation is not only a right of access; it carries duties of care toward other people, communities, the integrity of the system, and generations that may rely on what we create.

## 2. Our constitutional principles

### Human dignity is non-negotiable

Every person has inherent worth. No participant may be treated as disposable data, a source of extraction, a target for intimidation, or a means to another person’s power or profit.

We welcome serious disagreement, difficult questions, and rigorous critique. We do not accept dehumanization, humiliation, coercion, harassment, or attempts to silence people through fear, ridicule, exclusion, or economic pressure.

### Sovereignty includes responsibility

M5 recognizes the right of people and communities to exercise agency over their identity, assets, data, labor, knowledge, and lawful relationships. Sovereignty is not a license to dominate, deceive, exploit, or deny the dignity and rights of others.

In practice, this means respecting both individual autonomy and community authority. It means acknowledging that rights come with reciprocal duties: truthfulness, informed consent, stewardship, non-harm, due process, and accountability for one’s actions.

### Privacy is a right, not a product feature

We treat personal, financial, biometric, cultural, location, behavioral, and identity-related information as sensitive by default.

Participants must not collect, disclose, infer, sell, scrape, profile, retain, or repurpose another person’s information beyond a legitimate, clearly disclosed, authorized purpose. Consent must be meaningful, specific, revocable where feasible, and understandable to the person giving it.

No one may publish private messages, wallet details, personal addresses, legal names, credentials, account information, identity claims, security vulnerabilities, or other sensitive information without clear authorization or a lawful and urgent safety justification.

### Technology must serve living people

We build systems in which humans remain accountable for consequential decisions. Automation, algorithms, models, smart contracts, credentials, and AI agents must not become mechanisms for evading responsibility.

Where technology affects access to money, identity, opportunity, housing, employment, health, safety, legal standing, or community participation, builders and operators must prioritize explainability, recourse, proportionality, security, human review, and non-discrimination.

### Stewardship over extraction

We reject business practices that rely on hidden exploitation, predatory fees, unjust enrichment, deceptive design, re-hypothecation without informed authority, coercive surveillance, unauthorized data monetization, or the enclosure of community-created value.

Those who gain access to M5 infrastructure, capital, identity systems, registries, data, code, or community trust must use that access as a stewardship responsibility—not as a private entitlement.

### Peaceful pluralism and cultural respect

M5 operates across jurisdictions, traditions, languages, faiths, nations, states, tribes, communities, and legal systems. We recognize that no single institution, culture, or technical worldview has the right to erase another’s legitimate perspective.

We commit to peaceful coexistence, freedom of conscience, freedom of belief, and respect for lawful cultural and community authority. We do not require participants to abandon their identity, language, heritage, or community commitments in order to participate.

## 3. Expected conduct

All participants are expected to:

- Treat people, communities, and sovereign perspectives with dignity, even during fundamental disagreement.
- Critique ideas, designs, evidence, governance proposals, and implementations—not a person’s humanity, intelligence, identity, or right to participate.
- Communicate honestly. Distinguish facts, assumptions, opinions, estimates, marketing claims, and unresolved questions.
- Make room for people with different languages, abilities, resources, technical backgrounds, communication styles, legal traditions, and levels of digital access.
- Respect consent, privacy, confidentiality, cultural knowledge, and community authority.
- Seek informed permission before recording, publishing, tokenizing, training models on, commercializing, or otherwise using another person’s work, data, likeness, story, cultural material, or identity.
- Disclose material conflicts of interest, including financial interests, token holdings, governance roles, employment relationships, paid endorsements, licensing interests, family relationships, and incentives that could reasonably affect a decision or recommendation.
- Protect the security of the ecosystem by reporting vulnerabilities responsibly and avoiding public disclosure that creates foreseeable harm before remediation is possible.
- Use identity, credential, ledger, wallet, and registry systems lawfully, accurately, and in ways that preserve due process.
- Welcome correction when evidence shows that a claim, design, model, or action is flawed.
- Honor reasonable moderation, safety, and governance decisions while using established appeal processes when you believe a decision was incorrect or unjust.
- Act in good faith to repair harm when harm has occurred.

### Example: rigorous disagreement

**Acceptable:**

> “The proposed credential-revocation flow may create an unfair result because the member cannot review the evidence or appeal before access is restricted. I recommend a notice-and-response period, narrowly tailored emergency controls, and an independent review process.”

**Not acceptable:**

> “Anyone who designed this is corrupt, incompetent, and should be removed from the community.”

The first protects the system and invites improvement. The second attacks people rather than addressing the design, evidence, or remedy.

## 4. Special responsibilities

### Builders, operators, and technical contributors

People who create or operate M5 infrastructure hold a heightened duty of care because technical power can affect real lives.

You must:

- Design for security, resilience, accessibility, privacy, and reversibility where possible.
- Avoid hidden backdoors, deceptive telemetry, undisclosed data flows, manipulative user interfaces, and unauthorized privileged access.
- Document material limitations, dependencies, risks, security assumptions, and known conflicts.
- Avoid presenting experimental, unreviewed, or probabilistic systems as guaranteed, autonomous, legally determinative, or risk-free.
- Ensure AI agents, smart contracts, and automated workflows have defined human accountability, auditability, authority limits, and shutdown or escalation paths.
- Report vulnerabilities through responsible channels rather than exploiting them, selling them for harm, or exposing users to preventable risk.
- Respect open-source licenses, contributor rights, attribution, and the distinction between shared commons, protected intellectual property, and community-held knowledge.

### Financial and asset-system participants

M5-related systems may involve wallets, assets, settlement, registries, credentials, tokenization, identity, and financial tools. These areas require exceptional integrity.

You must not:

- Misrepresent an asset, title, ownership claim, reserve, collateral position, risk profile, legal status, liquidity, yield, or expected return.
- Promise guaranteed profits, fabricate endorsements, conceal fees, or use pressure tactics to induce financial decisions.
- Exploit information asymmetry, vulnerabilities, illiquidity, emergencies, or a person’s lack of technical knowledge.
- Use M5 systems for fraud, identity theft, market manipulation, money laundering, sanctions evasion, extortion, bribery, or other unlawful conduct.
- Treat a person’s wallet, credentials, data, or account as a source of control over them.
- Use financial access as leverage to coerce political, religious, sexual, cultural, or personal compliance.

### Governance participants and stewards

Power must be exercised with restraint, transparency, and accountability.

Stewards, moderators, trustees, administrators, reviewers, and decision-makers must:

- Disclose conflicts before participating in materially affected decisions.
- Give reasons for significant decisions whenever safety, privacy, or legal requirements permit.
- Apply standards consistently and avoid favoritism, retaliation, selective enforcement, or factional capture.
- Separate personal disputes from institutional decisions.
- Preserve records necessary for accountability while minimizing collection and exposure of personal data.
- Use the least restrictive effective response when addressing conduct concerns.
- Protect dissent, whistleblowing, and good-faith reporting from retaliation.
- Recuse themselves where they cannot make an impartial decision.

### Institutional and capital partners

Partnership with M5 carries a responsibility not to convert community trust into extractive control.

Partners must respect the ecosystem’s commitments to human agency, transparent economics, due process, data minimization, community benefit, and lawful sovereignty. Capital does not purchase the right to override member dignity, community governance, cultural authority, or the Foundation’s constitutional mission.

## 5. Prohibited conduct

The following conduct is prohibited in all M5 and TitleChain Foundation spaces, whether online, in person, public, private, technical, financial, legal, or social:

- Harassment, stalking, threats, intimidation, bullying, targeted humiliation, or incitement of violence.
- Hate speech, slurs, discriminatory treatment, or exclusion based on race, ethnicity, nationality, tribe, caste, religion, language, disability, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, immigration status, economic status, health status, or other protected or vulnerable status.
- Doxxing, non-consensual disclosure of private information, credential exposure, wallet tracing intended to endanger someone, or publication of confidential records.
- Sexual harassment, unwanted sexual attention, sexualized conduct in professional spaces, exploitation, or coercion.
- Fraud, deception, impersonation, credential forgery, false attestations, phishing, social engineering, identity theft, or misrepresentation of authority.
- Unauthorized access, malware, exploitation of vulnerabilities, theft of cryptographic keys, interference with systems, denial-of-service attacks, or attempts to compromise member or community security.
- Market manipulation, undisclosed paid promotion, pump-and-dump activity, deceptive token marketing, false claims of regulatory approval, or misrepresentation of asset ownership and rights.
- Coercive data collection, surveillance, scraping, profiling, biometric capture, or AI training using personal or community data without valid authorization.
- Retaliation against reporters, witnesses, complainants, security researchers, moderators, whistleblowers, or people who participate in a good-faith investigation.
- Cultural appropriation, extraction, or tokenization of traditional knowledge, community heritage, sacred material, or collective identity without the informed permission and participation of the appropriate knowledge holders or authorities.
- Attempts to capture governance, bypass safeguards, manipulate elections or consensus processes, purchase improper influence, or undermine constitutional limits for personal gain.
- Repeated bad-faith disruption, including sealioning, deliberate misinformation, coordinated harassment, spam, sabotage, or abuse of reporting systems.
- Using the language of sovereignty, freedom, decentralization, privacy, or constitutional rights to justify harm, domination, fraud, discrimination, or evasion of accountability.

## 6. Privacy, consent, and knowledge rights

Privacy and consent are central to the M5 vision. Participants should apply the following rules:

- Ask before sharing another person’s identity, image, voice, story, message, screen recording, meeting content, location, or financial information.
- Do not assume that public availability equals permission for commercial use, AI training, identity correlation, profiling, or permanent registry inclusion.
- Use data minimization: collect only what is necessary for a clear and legitimate purpose.
- Explain material uses of data in plain language before consent is requested.
- Respect a person’s right to ask questions, decline optional collection, correct information, and seek appropriate review of consequential records.
- Recognize that community-held, Indigenous, ancestral, sacred, and culturally sensitive knowledge may require collective consent, not merely individual permission.
- Treat children and other vulnerable persons with heightened care, safeguarding, and legal compliance.
- Never use privacy rights as a shield for fraud, abuse, credible threats, or harm to others.

## 7. Due process and fair participation

M5Bank and TitleChain Foundation support lawful order, human dignity, and fair process. In community governance and moderation, this means:

- People should receive notice of material allegations when doing so does not create a credible safety, legal, or evidence-preservation risk.
- Decisions should be based on relevant evidence rather than rumor, social status, wealth, technical influence, or factional loyalty.
- Responses should be proportionate to the seriousness, frequency, intent, impact, and repairability of the conduct.
- Where appropriate, participants should have an opportunity to respond, correct misunderstandings, repair harm, and appeal a significant decision.
- Emergency measures may be used when necessary to protect people, funds, systems, or evidence, but should be reviewed promptly.
- No person should be punished for good-faith criticism, principled dissent, respectful disagreement, or reporting legitimate concerns.
- Due process does not require the community to tolerate ongoing harm. Safety, privacy, and protection of vulnerable participants may require immediate limits on access or contact while a matter is reviewed.

## 8. Reporting concerns

If you experience or witness conduct that may violate this Code, report it through the designated M5Bank or TitleChain Foundation reporting channel.

A report should include, where available:

- What happened and why you believe it violates this Code.
- The date, time, location, platform, repository, event, wallet interaction, or relevant system.
- The identities or handles of people involved.
- Screenshots, messages, transaction references, logs, links, files, or other relevant evidence.
- Any immediate safety, security, financial, privacy, or retaliation concern.
- The outcome or protective measure you believe may be needed.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information will be shared only with people who need it to assess, investigate, protect participants, comply with law, or carry out a decision.

Do not post sensitive allegations, private evidence, or identifying information publicly unless disclosure is necessary for immediate safety or legally required. Public accusation can create additional harm, compromise an investigation, or expose vulnerable people to retaliation.

## 9. Investigation and response

The appropriate M5Bank or TitleChain Foundation stewarding body will review reports promptly, impartially, and with care for all affected parties.

Investigators and decision-makers should:

- Assess immediate risk and implement protective measures where necessary.
- Preserve confidentiality, privacy, and evidence integrity.
- Seek relevant information from affected parties and witnesses when appropriate.
- Disclose and manage conflicts of interest.
- Avoid retaliatory or prejudicial conduct.
- Document the basis for significant decisions.
- Communicate the outcome to relevant parties to the extent practical and safe.

The community may use one or more of the following responses:

| Response | When it may be appropriate |
| --- | --- |
| Clarification or coaching | A misunderstanding, lower-impact concern, or an opportunity for education and alignment |
| Private written warning | A clear violation that can be addressed through notice and behavior change |
| Required repair | An apology, correction, removal of harmful content, restitution, training, or a restorative process where safe and voluntary |
| Limited participation | Restrictions on specific channels, projects, events, privileges, or contact with particular people |
| Temporary suspension | Serious or repeated misconduct, pending investigation, or a safety-related need |
| Removal of role or authority | Abuse of trust, conflict of interest, security failure, governance misconduct, or inability to carry out responsibilities safely |
| Permanent exclusion | Severe misconduct, sustained harmful behavior, fraud, credible threats, exploitation, or violations that cannot be safely remedied |
| Referral to appropriate authorities | Conduct involving credible threats, serious fraud, exploitation, criminal activity, or legal obligations to report |

This approach is designed to repair harm where possible while retaining the ability to remove access decisively where safety, integrity, or trust requires it. Established community-enforcement models similarly emphasize confidentiality, impact-based responses, escalating consequences, and clear accountability.

## 10. Non-retaliation and appeals

Retaliation is prohibited.

No one may threaten, punish, exclude, harass, disadvantage, expose, or otherwise retaliate against a person because they:

- Reported a concern in good faith.
- Participated as a witness.
- Assisted an investigation.
- Raised a security or privacy issue responsibly.
- Disagreed with a decision respectfully.
- Requested an accommodation, clarification, recusal, or appeal.

A person directly affected by a material enforcement decision may request review through the designated appeal process. Appeals should identify a material procedural error, new relevant evidence, conflict of interest, disproportionate outcome, or other substantial reason to reconsider the decision.

Appeals are not an opportunity to relitigate an outcome simply because a participant dislikes it. The appeal reviewer must be appropriately independent from the original decision where practical.

## 11. Living covenant

This Code is a living covenant, not a branding document.

M5Bank and TitleChain Foundation will review it periodically with community input, especially as the ecosystem expands across jurisdictions, technologies, cultures, and forms of governance. Changes should preserve the core commitments of human dignity, privacy, consent, due process, non-extraction, accountable innovation, cultural respect, and stewardship for future generations.

No policy can anticipate every circumstance. Where the Code is silent, participants should choose the path that most faithfully protects human dignity, voluntary participation, privacy, safety, truthfulness, community trust, and the long-term constitutional purpose of the ecosystem.
