# Human-First Technology and Due Process

> **Publication status:** Adopted Version 1.0  
> **Adopted by:** TitleChain Foundation authorized governance body  
> **Effective date:** 2026-08-28  
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-08-28  
> **Next scheduled review:** 2027-08-28  
> **Legal review:** Approved for publication by authorized counsel on 2026-08-28.

## 1. Human authority over technology

M5-compatible systems are designed on the principle that technology serves human beings and legitimate organizations.

A person or authorized organization remains the source of authority over its identity, credentials, private data, wallet relationships, digital-asset relationships, connected devices, AI agents, and delegated actions, subject to applicable law and valid agreements.

Technology is a tool. It is not the ultimate authority over the person.

## 2. Human and digital-asset authority

A participant may retain meaningful authority over eligible identity relationships, credentials, data, records, delegated digital actions, and eligible digital-asset relationships.

A digital-asset relationship may include a bounded authority to access, hold, administer, sign for, present, receive, transfer, safeguard, recover, or otherwise manage a supported cryptographic wallet, account, tokenized record, digital asset, credential, asset identifier, or related signing capability.

A system MUST distinguish among:

- The subject of a record
- An owner, beneficial owner, controller, custodian, administrator, or authorized representative
- A technical key holder, wallet controller, signer, recovery contact, or hardware-device holder
- An issuer, attester, registrar, broker, exchange, custodian, or service provider
- A delegated agent
- A verifier, relying party, registry, resolver, or network operator

Possession or control of a key, wallet address, device, account, platform credential, or network address does not by itself establish legal identity, beneficial ownership, legal title, authority to transfer, custody status, fiduciary authority, regulatory status, or entitlement.

## 3. Credentialed proof of presence

M5 may support credentialed proof-of-presence, proof-of-life, identity, eligibility, authority, relationship, or status signals where appropriate to a defined use case.

A **Credentialed Proof of Presence** is a purpose-limited, privacy-preserving signal that a participant, authorized representative, or eligible device completed a defined presence, liveness, authentication, or interaction check under a stated policy.

It MUST be limited by its stated purpose, technical method, assurance level, retention period, privacy controls, and permitted relying-party context.

It does not establish:

- Universal legal identity
- Citizenship, residency, age, or legal capacity
- Legal ownership or title
- Legal authority or agency
- Eligibility for regulated services
- Consent to surveillance, tracking, profiling, or broad data collection

## 4. Connected devices and implants

A participant may voluntarily associate eligible connected devices, wearables, hardware security devices, assistive technologies, or implantable devices with an M5-compatible environment.

No participant should be required to use an implant, biometric, wearable, location-tracking device, neural interface, or other sensitive device as a general condition of human dignity, ordinary participation, or access to basic account functions unless a specific lawful and proportionate use case requires it and appropriate consent, accessibility, privacy, and alternative-access safeguards are available.

Data from connected devices or implants is sensitive. It MUST NOT be collected, accessed, disclosed, retained, linked, or used except under a defined purpose, authorized scope, applicable law, and valid consent or another lawful basis.

## 5. Accountable AI agents

An M5 Agent is a technical delegate, not an independent sovereign authority.

Each M5 Agent MUST have:

- A Responsible Sponsor
- A unique agent identity
- Verified current authority scope
- Effective dates and revocation conditions
- Applicable policy and risk classification
- Material-action records or receipts
- Escalation rules for high-impact actions
- Enhanced safeguards where required

An M5 Agent MUST NOT make a legally binding, irreversible, high-impact, or rights-affecting decision without the authority, controls, review, and human involvement required by applicable law, policy, and the relevant service arrangement.

## 6. Human review and contestability

Where an automated process materially affects a member’s access, credentials, authority, digital-asset relationship, service eligibility, or participation status, the independently responsible operator SHOULD provide a meaningful human review, correction, explanation, dispute, or escalation path where required by law, contract, or applicable policy.

## 7. Due process serves the human first

M5-compatible systems SHOULD use attributable, credentialed, documented, and reviewable processes for material requests affecting a member’s rights, access, credentials, agent authority, wallet relationship, or digital-asset relationship.

A technical event, algorithmic score, external claim, automated flag, platform instruction, or unverified demand MUST NOT by itself determine a member’s rights or authorize access to private data or value.

Where a system provides notice routing, it may authenticate, timestamp, and route a notice to a member-controlled Notice Channel. Notice routing does not determine legal effect, legal jurisdiction, the merits of a request, or the legal rights of the parties.

## 8. No unaccountable override

No technology provider, platform operator, automated system, or agent may use this framework to establish unaccountable control over a participant’s identity, personal data, private communications, wallet relationships, devices, agents, or digital-asset relationships.
