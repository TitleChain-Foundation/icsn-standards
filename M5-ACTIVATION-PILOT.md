# M5 Activation Pilot

> **Status: Public Review Draft for the people's review.** This document describes a proposed activation and learning sequence. It does not represent an account, credential, payment service, business registration, employment offer or guarantee.

## Pilot purpose

The first curriculum pilot exists to activate the people, roles and workflows required to operate the M5Ecosystem and then teach the same consent-based pathway to other members. It is an implementation pilot before it is a broad course catalog.

This pilot is the prioritized implementation subset of the broader [Founding Roles and Activation Pathway](FOUNDING-ROLES-AND-ACTIVATION.md). The founding-role map describes long-term strategic capabilities and independent control functions; this pilot sequences the roles needed first to reproduce and teach one complete member activation journey. Where titles differ, the workstream and accountable authority matter more than the title.

The pilot should validate one complete reference journey across TitleChain Foundation, M5Capital and the member-controlled M5 stack:

1. IAM activation;
2. BOM waitlist and, after separate approval, BOM activation;
3. an eligible BOU pathway and/or an existing or newly registered entity using a BOB pathway;
4. M5POD and Sovereign Shell deployment;
5. holder-controlled credentials and presentations;
6. M5isYOU role, learning and service profile;
7. a Ricardian service contract;
8. an invoice or payment request;
9. receipt or settlement through a supported route; and
10. launch of a member-controlled AI-native, on-chain business.

The M5isYOU profile is created or affirmatively approved by the person and remains under that person's control. The pilot may provide tools, prompts and assistance, but must not generate or publish a profile as though the Foundation created it for the member. Verified credentials validate only specified claims; they do not replace holder approval or transfer control of the profile.

## First reference implementation

Pamela may serve as the first named reference participant only to the extent she expressly consents. Public documentation may describe the sequence, decisions, templates and redacted evidence. It must not publish identity documents, authentication factors, wallet addresses, private account identifiers, credentials, POD contents, contracts, invoices or transaction data without separate, specific disclosure approval.

No account or credential should be described as activated, issued or verified until the accountable issuer or operator has recorded the supporting evidence. The public pilot should distinguish `planned`, `in progress`, `activated`, `verified`, `suspended` and `revoked` states.

## Activation job groups

The job groups below organize the initial implementation work. They do not replace the detailed founding-role descriptions or the searchable Sophia catalog. One qualified person may support more than one early workstream only when conflicts are disclosed and required independent approvals remain separated.

### 1. Canon and architecture

- Chief or principal M5 architect
- M5Canon and sovereign-purpose alignment steward
- Systems and integration architect
- Product and technical program manager
- Technical documentation and decision-record steward

This group maintains architectural boundaries, provenance, interfaces and change control so the implementation does not create unmanaged technical debt or unreviewed agent authority.

### 2. IAM, identity and credentials

- IAM product and implementation lead
- Identity lifecycle engineer
- Applied cryptographer
- Credential schema and issuer engineer
- Proof-of-humanity, consent and recovery specialist
- Wallet credential and selective-presentation engineer

### 3. Member accounts and onboarding operations

This group packages and deploys the member-controlled M5POD and Shell across supported devices and validates consent, portability, recovery and local-agent boundaries.

## Train-the-onboarder sequence

Every instructor, mentor and onboarding operator should complete the member journey before leading it for others:

1. understand consent, privacy and the public/private boundary;
2. begin and complete the applicable IAM activation steps;
3. join the BOM waitlist and understand that it is not an active payment account;
4. deploy and recover their M5POD and Shell;
5. receive, store and selectively present a training credential;
6. complete an M5isYOU profile and role selection;
7. prepare and sign a sandbox Ricardian service contract;
8. issue a sandbox invoice or payment request;
9. test an approved settlement adapter without exposing payment secrets; and
10. demonstrate how to support another person without taking control of that person's identity, wallet, POD or business.

## Curriculum produced from the pilot

Each validated step becomes a reusable module containing:

- a human-readable guide;
- a machine-readable workflow;
- accountable roles and authority limits;
- prerequisites and evidence;
- privacy and security controls;
- a sandbox exercise;
- a human-reviewed demonstration;
- a credential definition and issuer policy;
- recovery and appeal procedures; and
- a versioned instructor guide.

## Completion outcomes

A participant may leave the pilot with holder-controlled evidence that they completed specific modules, demonstrated specific capabilities, taught or mentored a module, or launched an AI-native business workflow. These are distinct claims and must not be collapsed into a universal M5 score.

Business launch evidence may reference an entity, service offer, signed contract, invoice and settlement receipt through selective presentations. It does not imply profitability, regulatory approval, investment suitability or Foundation endorsement.

## Pilot readiness gate

The pathway should not open to a broader cohort until the reference implementation has:

- documented each account state and responsible operator;
- tested consent, correction, export and recovery;
- verified the credential issuance and presentation flow;
- completed a sandbox contract, invoice and settlement cycle;
- passed security, privacy and legal review appropriate to the pilot;
- produced instructor and learner guides; and
- recorded unresolved risks and manual fallback procedures.
