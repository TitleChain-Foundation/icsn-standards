Dear Ms. Countryman:
On behalf of TitleChain Foundation, I respectfully submit the enclosed public comment in
response to the Securities and Exchange Commission's proposed Transfer Agent Rules, File No.
S7-2026-30, Release No. 34-106246.
TitleChain Foundation is a public-commons foundation developing open, interoperable reference
standards for title, provenance, accountable digital identity, machine-readable authority,
distributed-ledger record-keeping, and human-governed automation.
Our comment is directed specifically to modernizing transfer-agent infrastructure as securities
records move from paper and institution-specific databases toward electronic records, distributed
ledgers, digital assets, and secure AI-assisted operations designed to reduce rogue-agent
exposure by preventing an automated agent from becoming the source of its own authority.
The Foundation's principal recommendation is that the Commission encourage voluntary,
implementation-neutral open standards that allow regulated entities to interoperate without
surrendering control of their legally authoritative records or regulatory responsibilities. The
comment addresses, among other matters:
· the distinction between a wallet or digital identifier and legally accountable authority;
· credential-bound human and entity authority for digital transfer instructions;
· machine-readable restrictive legends and transfer conditions;
· a Ricardian three-part representation of human-readable terms, machine-readable meaning,
and separately bounded executable instructions;

· pre-activation controls for automated agents using the M5Canon Six-Gate model;
· separation of origination/minting authority from later transfer, legend, correction, and
cancellation authority;
· preservation of the underlying Title Rights & Provenance Record across digital wrappers,
transfer systems, and settlement rails; and
· open, publicly reviewable reference architectures that can coexist with the legally
authoritative records maintained by registered transfer agents.
The Foundation does not ask the Commission to designate TitleChain, M5Canon, or any other
registry as a required system, and does not propose that a public registry supersede a registered
transfer agent's official books and records. We offer the architecture solely as an optional public
reference model for interoperability, auditability, portability, and accountable automation.
Public information about the Foundation is available at https://titlechainfoundation.org. The
Foundation's public standards commons is available at https://github.com/TitleChain-
Foundation/icsn-standards.
Thank you for the opportunity to comment on this important modernization effort.
