I. Core thesis
The Commission's proposal appropriately modernizes transfer-agent rules and forms for "the
technological environment in which transfer agents operate, including the widespread use of
electronic record-keeping and communications, and the services they provide to issuers,

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 4 of 21
investors, and other market intermediaries" (Federal Register). The proposal amends a broad set
of existing transfer-agent rules (including Rules 17ad-1, 17ad-2, 17ad-3, 17ad-6, 17ad-7, 17ad-9,
17ad-10, 17ad-12, 17ad-13, and 17ad-17), rescinds Rule 17ad-4, adopts two new rules (Rule
17ad-30, Compliance, and Rule 17ad-31, Restrictive legends), and amends Forms TA-1 and
TA-2 and the registration rules 17ac2-1 and 17ac2-2. The proposal reflects that a registered
transfer agent may, under existing staff guidance, use distributed-ledger technology as its official
Master Security holder File while remaining subject to federal securities-law requirements
concerning record-keeping, reporting, examinations, and operational obligations (Federal
Register).
The Foundation respectfully submits that this modernization is necessary but incomplete in one
respect: it presupposes a neutral public reference architecture for title, identity, authority,
jurisdiction, credentialing, and auditability that the rule text does not yet provide. Without
such a reference architecture, the proposal leaves each transfer agent to construct its own
proprietary approach to DLT-based Master Security holder Files, wallet-based investor
identifiers, machine-readable restrictive legends, and automated processing. The result would be
fragmentation, inconsistent investor protection, and reduced interoperability — the very harms
the proposal seeks to remedy.
The Foundation therefore recommends that the Commission include in the adopting release, or in
supplementary guidance, a request for comment and a direction toward open, interoperable,
credential-bound, jurisdiction-aware DLT record-keeping standards for transfer agents. The
remainder of this comment identifies the specific elements such a reference architecture should
contain and ties each to the provisions of the proposal.
II. Open standards, public-commons infrastructure, and interoperability
TitleChain Foundation is a public-commons foundation committed to developing and
maintaining an open-source, decentralized reference architecture for title, provenance,
authority, and ownership records. The Foundation's M5Canon framework provides a
standards-based governance and policy layer intended to support infrastructure that is
interoperable, auditable, portable, resilient, and capable of being implemented by many
independent organizations rather than controlled by a single platform.
The Foundation's public materials describe a long-term stewardship and non-capture model
anchored by the TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust, designed for long-duration public
stewardship and intended to reduce the risk that foundational infrastructure becomes controlled
by any single company, government, institution, vendor, or private interest (TitleChain
Foundation).
The Foundation submits this architecture solely as an optional, publicly reviewable reference
model. We do not ask the Commission to designate TitleChain, M5Canon, or any other registry
as a required system; to appoint the Foundation as an operator or administrator of regulated

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 5 of 21
records; or to permit any public or decentralized registry to supersede the official books and
records that applicable law assigns to a registered transfer agent, issuer, or other regulated entity.
Instead, we recommend that the Commission encourage the development and voluntary adoption
of open, interoperable, machine-readable standards for distributed-ledger and digital-asset
record-keeping. Such standards could allow transfer agents, issuers, custodians, broker-dealers,
regulated trading venues, financial institutions, and other authorized service providers to
exchange verifiable information concerning identity, authority, title, provenance, restrictions,
ownership state, and permitted transfers without requiring every participant to operate within the
same proprietary system.
Under this model, each regulated entity would continue to control and remain responsible for its
own legally authoritative records, compliance obligations, supervisory procedures, security
controls, governance, and regulated functions. Open standards would provide the
interoperability layer between those systems, not replace the institutions or legal
responsibilities that govern them.
This distinction becomes increasingly important as markets move from paper and institution-
specific databases toward programmable records, distributed ledgers, digital assets, and AI-
assisted operations. Without common standards, the market risks replacing today's fragmented
databases with a new generation of incompatible proprietary ledgers and platform-controlled
digital records.
The Foundation therefore proposes a different path: a durable public technical commons in
which independent registries and regulated systems can interoperate while authority remains
distributed among the legally responsible parties.
In practical terms, the reference model is designed to allow:
> one issuer to use one system, a registered transfer agent another, a custodian another,
and a broker or regulated venue another—while each can verify the same underlying title,
provenance, authority, restrictions, and state-transition evidence without surrendering
control of its own authoritative records.
Planned decentralized commons governance
The Foundation's present legal and operating entities will remain responsible for their existing
obligations while the public-commons infrastructure is being built. Upon funding of the team
required to support the standards program, activation of the required member base, adoption of
governing principles, and completion of appropriate legal, tax, and regulatory review, the
Foundation intends to organize a separate Wyoming Decentralized Unincorporated
Nonprofit Association (DUNA) for the decentralized stewardship and member governance of
eligible TitleChain/ICSN public-commons activities.
Wyoming's DUNA framework requires at least 100 members joined by mutual consent for a
common nonprofit purpose and permits governing principles to include agreements, consensus

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 6 of 21
mechanisms, smart contracts, and enacted governance proposals. The contemplated DUNA
would provide a member-governed vehicle for eligible commons activities; it would not cause
software or autonomous agents to become legal decision-makers, eliminate accountable human
authority, alter the duties of regulated participants, or automatically absorb existing TitleChain or
M5Labs entities.
Consistent with the M5HUM/M5AGT authority model, membership alone would not confer
operational authority. Administrator powers, regulated roles, credentials, delegated agent
permissions, and executable actions would remain separately defined, scoped, revocable, and
auditable under the governing principles, applicable law, and M5Canon controls. TitleChain
Foundation Labs (M5Labs), operating from Beverly Hills, California, is intended to support
engineering, research, standards implementation, and program operations while remaining
responsible for its own legal and operating obligations unless a later restructuring is separately
approved and completed.
The DUNA roadmap is a future governance plan and is not represented in this comment as
already formed or activated.
The objective is not to centralize authority or remove regulated intermediaries. It is to provide a
shared public language for trustworthy digital records so that the transition from paper to
digital assets strengthens, rather than weakens, provenance, accountability, portability,
competition, and regulatory visibility.
For public and regulatory materials, this comment uses Title Rights & Provenance Record as
the plain-language name for the structured TitleChain record that preserves the underlying right,
provenance, authority, restrictions, lifecycle, and linked evidence. The internal M5/TitleChain
architecture may refer to the same construct as the Title Container.
III. M5IAM / M5HUM and credential-bound participant identifiers
Commissioner Peirce has specifically asked whether transfer-agent rules should continue to
require names and physical addresses or allow "other identifiers, such as email and digital
wallet addresses" as securities move onchain (SEC statement, Sept. 1, 2026).
The Foundation respectfully submits that a bare wallet address is an identifier, not authority.
A wallet address alone does not establish the accountable person or legal entity, the person's
current role, jurisdiction, authority scope, professional or organizational standing, revocation
status, or the evidence supporting a transfer instruction.
The M5 reference architecture separates these functions:
M5IAM
  ↓
TCID / M5HUM — accountable human principal
  ↓
M5POD — holder-controlled private identity / credential evidence
  ↓

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 7 of 21
M5-CV / SOPHIA — selected capability, role, and credential state
  ↓
credential-bound wallet / delegated M5AGT
  ↓
M5Canon authority evaluation
M5IAM is the identity-and-access floor. M5HUM identifies the accountable human principal.
M5-CV/SOPHIA may describe current capability and role evidence, while any delegated
M5AGT remains subordinate to the current authority of its accountable M5HUM or legal entity.
No capability profile, credential, wallet, or agent becomes an independent source of legal
authority.
This model does not replace customer-identification, transfer-agent registration, issuer records,
sanctions controls, professional licensing, or any securities-law obligation. It provides a
standards-based way to bind a digital identifier to purpose-limited, current, revocable authority
without requiring the wallet address itself to carry legal meaning.
Specific recommendation: The Commission should invite comment on whether a credential-
bound participant-identifier model; including accountable-principal identity, current
role/credential state, jurisdiction, revocation, and evidence would improve the reliability of DLT-
based transfer-agent record-keeping alongside existing investor-identification obligations.
IV. Known entity, current human authority, and jurisdiction; Forms TA-1
and TA-2
The proposal would amend Forms TA-1 and TA-2; amend Rule 17ac2-1 to change the
registration effectiveness period; and amend Rule 17ac2-2 to require correction of materially
inaccurate, misleading, or incomplete Form TA-2 information within the proposed period after
discovery (Federal Register).
For modern digital workflows, the Foundation recommends separating entity resolution from
human/agent authority. A transfer-agent system should be able to establish the legal entity or
institution first, then determine which person currently has authority to act for it, and only then
determine whether an automated agent is permitted to perform the exact requested function.

The broader M5 reference implementation includes JNR for jurisdiction resolution and
SwiftBRIDGE for cross-referencing legacy institutional identifiers and account/routing
relationships to M5 entity/account references. Those layers do not create governmental or
regulatory authority and do not replace the authoritative issuer of a BIC, LEI, government
identifier, professional license, or transfer-agent registration.
The significance for Forms TA-1 and TA-2 is practical: changes in registration, employment,
appointment, role, jurisdiction, or good standing can be recorded as current-state changes
without erasing the identity of the person or the historical evidence of prior authorized actions.
Specific recommendation: The Commission should invite comment on whether Forms TA-1
and TA-2 should elicit, in a technology-neutral manner, information about DLT Master
Securityholder Files, credential-bound identifiers, delegated/automated agents, jurisdictional
controls, and revocation/correction governance.
V. Machine-readable restrictive legends and transfer rights
The proposal would adopt new Rule 17ad-31 concerning restrictive legends, including
maintenance of a current list of issuer employees authorized to instruct a transfer agent regarding
placement and removal of legends and controls against acting on unauthorized instructions
(Federal Register).
The Foundation recommends that restrictive legends and transfer conditions be representable as
machine-readable policy objects bound to the underlying Title Rights & Provenance
Record, rather than existing only as text attached to one token representation. Where those
conditions are expressed through an executable agreement, the Foundation further recommends a
Ricardian three-part representation consisting of: (1) human-readable legal terms, (2)
machine-readable structured meaning, and (3) separately bounded executable code or
instructions. All three representations should share the same agreement identifier, version, and
content digest, with an explicit conflict rule providing that executable code may not silently alter
the controlling human-readable legal terms or the legally authoritative transfer-agent record.
A machine-readable restriction should be able to identify its legal/source authority, jurisdiction,
affected asset or issue, current status, permitted actors, placement/removal authority, effective
period, required approvals, and evidence. When a wrapper, bridge, wallet, or execution rail
changes, the restriction should remain attached to the underlying right and authoritative record
relationship.

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 9 of 21
This is also where authority must be granular. Permission to originate or mint a representation
does not automatically confer permission to transfer it, approve a transfer, remove a legend,
correct a record, freeze/hold the asset, or terminate the representation.
The M5 reference architecture uses M5MST — Origination, Minting, Sovereign Registration,
and Transfer Authority for the controlled origination/mint/registration state-event: the event
records who had authority to originate the M5AST representation and the evidence supporting
that authority. Subsequent transfer capabilities are separately scoped and revocable.
Specific recommendation: The Commission should invite comment on whether a standards-
based, machine-readable restrictive-legend and transfer-right format, bound to the Title Rights &
Provenance Record rather than to a token alone, would improve transfer restriction enforcement
and examination evidence.
VI. M5Canon Six-Gate control for automated and AI-assisted transfer-agent
workflows
The proposal would adopt new Rule 17ad-30, requiring registered transfer agents to establish,
maintain, and enforce written compliance policies and procedures, and would replace Rule
17ad-12 with a broader risk-management framework addressing theft, loss, misuse, unauthorized
access, and business continuity (Federal Register).

execution control model in which an automated agent cannot become the source of its own
authority.

This model can be applied before an M5AGT is activated for a transfer-agent function, not
merely after the action for surveillance. The agent's permitted authority is the intersection of the
principal's current authority, delegated scope, current credentials, jurisdiction, asset/issue policy,
restrictions, approval state, and valid time window.
The architecture is designed to reduce rogue-agent exposure by preventing a wallet, model, or
API key from conferring authority by itself. It does not eliminate the transfer agent's
responsibility for its regulated functions; it makes the source, scope, approval, and evidence of
machine action explicit and reconstructable.
The Foundation's public authorization-decision-receipt schema illustrates this pattern through
structured fields for principal, agent, operation, purpose, jurisdiction, policy version, decision,
human-approval requirements, expiry, and provenance. The Foundation's M5AGT operating
policy likewise requires a named accountable human/entity and prohibits the agent from
independently creating or extending authority.
Specific recommendation: The Commission should invite comment on whether the transfer-
agent compliance and risk-management framework should contemplate pre-activation controls
for automated agents covering accountable principal identity, current credential/appointment,
jurisdiction, deterministic policy, required approval, revocation, and auditable evidence.
VII. Preserving the underlying right while wrappers and legal/regulatory
state change
The Foundation's technical state model distinguishes the underlying asset/right from later
financial-wrapper and jurisdictional/security-state information.
For public reference purposes, the internal M5 model uses:
· M1 — Money + Utility;
· M2 — Commodities;
· M3 — Titled / Unique / Collectible RWA;
· M4 — Financial Wrapper; and
· M5 — Jurisdictional Security-State Profile.
The important transfer-agent point is not the labels themselves. It is the persistence rule: later
wrapper, restriction, credential, correction, settlement, or legal/regulatory state must not
erase the underlying Title Rights & Provenance Record or its history.
External legal/regulatory classification remains separate and authoritative. M5Canon may
evaluate and enforce the resulting state and restrictions; an internal M5 label does not determine
whether an instrument or transaction is legally a security, commodity, investment contract, or
other regulated arrangement.

TITLECHAIN FOUNDATION  |  SEC FILE NO. S7-2026-30 Page 11 of 21
This separation allows a transfer agent to preserve provenance and authoritative-record linkage
while recording changes in legends, holder eligibility, financial representation, settlement rail,
and regulatory state over time.
The broader M5Global architecture also includes a proposed M5 Global Index and Exchange
public economic-intelligence layer for privacy-preserving, provenance-aware indicators and
methodologies. That layer is an economic data/index exchange and is not a securities trading
venue and is not necessary to the transfer-agent functions requested in this comment.
Specific recommendation: The Commission should invite comment on whether a reference
state model that separates the underlying right from wrapper and legal/regulatory state would
improve provenance, restriction, correction, and settlement-state preservation across technology
changes.
VIII. Requested outcome
The Foundation respectfully requests that the Commission, in the adopting release or in
supplementary guidance:
1. Invite comment on voluntary open-standards reference architectures for DLT-based
Master Security holder Files (proposed Rules 17ad-9, 17ad-6, 17ad-7), including an underlying
Title Rights & Provenance Record with append-only lifecycle history and explicit linkage to the
legally authoritative record.
2. Recognize, for comment, a credential-bound participant-identifier model in which a wallet
address is bound to an accountable principal, current role/credential, jurisdiction, scope,
revocation state, and evidence — alongside, not in lieu of, existing obligations.
3. Consider whether Forms TA-1 and TA-2 should elicit technology-neutral information about
DLT Master Security holder Files, credentialed identifiers, delegated/automated agents,
jurisdictional controls, and revocation/correction governance.
4. Consider machine-readable restrictive legends and transfer-right objects bound to the
underlying Title Rights & Provenance Record rather than to a token alone.
5. Consider, within proposed Rules 17ad-30 and 17ad-12, a pre-activation automated-agent
control model covering accountable principal identity, current role/credential, jurisdiction,
deterministic policy, required human/entity approval, revocation, and tamper-evident evidence.
6. Invite comment on an origination-versus-transfer authority model, so the person/entity
authorized to create or register a digital representation is not presumed to possess every later
transfer, legend, correction, or cancellation capability.
7. Invite comment on a reference state model that separates the underlying right from financial
wrappers and transaction-specific legal/regulatory state for provenance and record preservation.
The Foundation does not request that any specific standard, registry, or provider be mandated or
designated, and does not seek to perform any regulated transfer-agent function itself. The

Appendix A — Glossary of commenter terms (proposed, not authoritative)
These are the Foundation's reference-architecture terms, not established legal categories and not
substitutes for applicable law.
· Title Rights & Provenance Record (internal architecture term: Title Container) —
structured record of the underlying right/asset, provenance, lifecycle, restrictions, authority,
and related evidence; it does not displace the legally authoritative transfer-agent record where
applicable.
· Ricardian three-part representation — synchronized human-readable legal terms,
machine-readable structured meaning, and separately bounded executable code/instructions
sharing a common identifier, version, and digest; executable code does not silently override
the controlling legal text or authoritative record.
· Planned DUNA commons governance — contemplated future Wyoming member-governed
vehicle for eligible public-commons activities after funding, membership, governing-
principles, and legal/tax/regulatory requirements are satisfied; not represented as currently
formed or activated.
· M5IAM — identity-and-access floor of the M5 reference implementation.
· M5HUM — accountable human principal type in the M5 reference implementation.
· M5AGT — bounded autonomous/software agent acting under delegated authority and never
exceeding the current authority of its accountable principal.
· M5AST — digital asset/token representation associated with an underlying TitleChain
record; not presumed to be the underlying legal asset or authoritative ownership record.
· M5MST — Origination, Minting, Sovereign Registration, and Transfer Authority; records
who had authority at origination and which later transfer, restriction, legend, correction, or
termination capabilities are separately permitted.
· M5Canon Six Gates — principal identity, current role/credential, jurisdiction, deterministic
policy, required accountable approval, and evidence/audit anchoring.
· JNR — jurisdiction naming/resolution layer that maps technical namespace references to
authoritative external jurisdictional sources; it does not create governmental authority.
· SwiftBRIDGE — legacy financial-institution identifier/account crosswalk to M5
jurisdiction/account references; it is not the SWIFT network.

Appendix D — Technical Definitions, Standards Alignment, and Minimum
Conformance Objects
Purpose and boundary. This appendix supplies enough technical detail to evaluate the
Foundation’s recommendations without requiring a reviewer to follow external links. It describes
an implementation-neutral reference model. It does not claim that any M5 component is
registered, licensed, certified, endorsed, integrated, deployed, audited, or legally sufficient for a
regulated activity.
Meaning of standards alignment. In this comment, “aligned,” “compatible,” and “supported”
mean that the architecture is designed to map to, transport, reference, or validate data expressed
through an external specification. Those terms do not assert full conformance, certification,
formal partnership, trademark permission, or endorsement. Applicable law, official registries,
regulated entities, and authoritative records remain controlling.
D.1 Authority is not execution
A cryptographic key, wallet address, account identifier, API credential, software agent, or model
endpoint can identify or authenticate a technical actor, but it does not by itself establish current
legal authority. Authority must be derived from an accountable human or legal entity and
evaluated against current role, credential, jurisdiction, purpose, scope, restrictions, approvals,
expiry, suspension, and revocation state.
An automated agent may prepare, route, or execute an instruction only within delegated authority
already established by an accountable principal. The agent may not issue its own credential,
expand its own scope, waive an approval, select a different jurisdiction, or treat successful
technical execution as proof of legal authorization. This separation reduces rogue-agent exposure
by preventing the agent from becoming the source of its own authority.
D.2 Credential-bound participant and authority tuple
A transfer instruction should resolve, directly or by authoritative reference, the following
minimum tuple: principal identifier; represented entity; current role or appointment; credential

D.4 M5Pay and payment-rail neutrality
M5Pay is an implementation component beneath the M5-OMI interoperability boundary and the
M5Canon authority layer. It is intended to be rail-, asset-, provider-, ledger-, wallet-, and
protocol-agnostic. It may translate and route an already-authorized payment or settlement
instruction to an eligible external rail, but it does not create the principal’s authority, determine
legal classification, become the authoritative securities record, or displace the regulated
institution responsible for custody, money movement, settlement, recordkeeping, or compliance.
Depending on the transaction and responsible provider, an adapter could support bank and
payment messaging, card or account-to-account rails, approved digital-asset settlement, or
HTTP-native machine-payment protocols. Each adapter must declare its provider, jurisdiction,
asset and account classifications, credentials, limits, finality semantics, error handling,
reconciliation method, and evidence output. Support for an adapter or message format does not
imply that every instrument or rail is legally available in every context.

D.6 Examination, portability, and fail-closed behavior
A conforming implementation should permit an examiner or authorized reviewer to reconstruct
why an action was allowed, who approved it, which policy and credential versions controlled,
what authoritative state changed, and how the result reconciled to required books and records.
Evidence access should be role-appropriate and should not require indiscriminate publication of
personal, privileged, confidential, or security-sensitive information.
Portability requires open, documented exports and independently verifiable checksums,
signatures, schema versions, restriction state, credential/status references, and unresolved
exceptions. If an identity, credential, jurisdiction, policy, approval, or authoritative-state
dependency is unavailable, inconsistent, expired, revoked, or outside scope, consequential
execution should fail closed or route to accountable review rather than allowing an agent or
adapter to infer its own authority.
