# Membership and Repository Roles

This document clarifies participation in the public ICSN standards repository. It complements [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) and does not replace the TitleChain Foundation's legal bylaws or board resolutions.

## Public participation roles

- **Follower or stargazer:** signals public interest by starring the repository or following Foundation activity.
- **Watcher:** receives repository notifications according to their GitHub settings.
- **Participant:** joins Discussions, public reviews or community events.
- **Contributor:** submits useful issues, RFCs, code, tests, research, documentation, translations or implementation evidence.

Anyone may begin through these public pathways, subject to the Code of Conduct and repository policies.

## Trusted repository roles

- **Organization member:** belongs to the GitHub organization and may receive access appropriate to a defined responsibility.
- **Editor:** helps specification text and RFCs meet repository requirements.
- **Maintainer:** manages repository operations, moderation, releases and assigned permissions.
- **Standards steward:** participates in recorded standards-maturity decisions under the governance process.

Trusted roles are based on sustained contribution, sound judgment, respectful participation, security awareness, disclosed conflicts and an actual operational need. Access should follow least-privilege principles and be reviewed when responsibilities change.

An invitation to the GitHub organization is not automatic. It should identify the role, scope, responsible maintainer and any time or review conditions.

## What these roles do not create

Starring, watching, participating, contributing or receiving a GitHub organization role does not by itself create:

- an M5BankOfMe or IAM account;
- a sovereign identity, credential or proof-of-humanity status;
- employment, compensation or a funding commitment;
- authority to bind or speak legally for the TitleChain Foundation;
- standards approval, certification or conformance status;
- ownership of community, member or Foundation data; or
- authority from any government, international organization or public agency.

Any such status requires its own documented, consent-based and authorized process.

## Cohort, funding and compensated work

Organization members who maintain a constructive contribution record may be eligible to apply for separately announced M5Bank contributor cohorts, mission grants or compensated assignments. Eligibility creates an opportunity to apply—not an account, award, token allocation or payment entitlement.

Selection and payment must follow documented criteria, conflicts review and the appropriate grant, employment, contractor, bounty or procurement process. A person must not participate in approving their own award. Foundation authority, repository permissions and standards decisions must remain independent of financial support and compensation.

See the [Cohort, Funding and Contributor Rewards Framework](COHORT-FUNDING-AND-CONTRIBUTOR-REWARDS.md) for the proposed pathway, digital-asset controls, DUNA formation boundary and M5OpenAPI marketplace responsibilities.

The [Founding Roles and Activation Pathway](FOUNDING-ROLES-AND-ACTIVATION.md) describes anticipated operating capabilities as cohorts, registries and products develop. Publication in that map does not mean a role is funded, open or appointed.

## Public and private information boundary

The public repository may contain standards proposals, public governance records, implementation-neutral examples and contribution history.

Do not publish private keys, wallet data, identity documents, personal member records, credentials, financial records, production secrets, private agent controls, sensitive threat information or vulnerability details. Use the private security-reporting process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) where appropriate.

No draft charter-member list or implementation-side account registry should be treated as the Foundation's public membership roster unless it has been separately verified, authorized and published through the Foundation's governance process.

## Path toward organization membership

1. Star or watch the repository and become familiar with its policies.
2. Introduce yourself and your area of focus in Discussions.
3. Build a record of constructive contribution and collaboration.
4. Identify an ongoing responsibility that requires organization access.
5. Receive a scoped invitation from an authorized organization owner or maintainer.
6. Complete any required security, conflict-of-interest or role review.

Organization membership is a means of carrying responsibility—not a measure of a person's worth, identity or right to participate in the public standards process.
