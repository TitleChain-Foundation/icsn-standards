## What is in the repositories

The public commons currently includes:

**M5-OpenAPI specifications.** The connector surface. Every integration M5 supports is described as a spec first and an implementation second, so a third party can build against it without asking us for access.

**TitleChain schemas.** How a document becomes an asset. Title records, transfer attestations, adjudication and insurance metadata - the data structures that let a file carry provable ownership.

**CHI identity specification.** Constitutional Human Intelligence, the encasement around every account. Stack order is CYRUS -> TitleChain -> M5 OS Stack -> MILNER -> OpenFHE -> AAIF. Every account gets its own CHI, and the spec describes exactly what that means and what it forbids.

**Governance and charter documents.** How decisions get made, who can make them, and how you change that. Read this one first if you want to know whether the rest is real.

**Reference implementations.** Working examples against the specs, in the open, so the specification is never the only artifact.

**A good-first-issue backlog.** Curated, labeled, and small enough to finish in an evening. If you have never contributed to a standards project, start here.

## Time, talents, tokens

Everything counts right now, and the honest ranking is not the one you would expect. A reviewed specification is worth more to us this month than a large cheque.

**Write code.** Open a pull request. The good-first-issue backlog is the front door, and reference SDK work is where we are thinnest.

**Sponsor on GitHub.** Recurring sponsorship at any tier funds specification work and the maintainers who do it. [View the Foundation Sponsors page](https://github.com/sponsors/TitleChain-Foundation).

**Contribute in tokens.** For contributors who would rather fund the Foundation on-chain than through a card. Open an issue and we will publish the receiving addresses and attestation flow in the open, on the repository, where everyone can see them.

**Participate in governance.** Read the charter. Argue with it in an issue. The governance process is itself a public specification, and it improves the same way the rest of the work does - in public, with your name on it.

**Star and watch.** The lowest-cost thing on this list and not a small one. Visibility is how a standard gets adopted, and adoption is the only thing that makes a standard a standard.

## Why open, and why now

A closed specification is a promise. An open specification is a record.

We are asking members to put their identity, their money, their documents, and their agents inside this system they control. Nobody should do that on a promise. The specifications have to be readable by someone who does not trust us, and the governance has to be changeable by someone who is not us. That is the whole reason the Foundation exists as a separate body from M5Capital.

The work builds on the same open foundations we have always named: Matrix, Signal, Solid, Tauri, OpenStack, AAIF, Eclipse, OpenHW, iMasons, and the 23andMine Foundation. We are adding to that lineage, not replacing it.

The Foundation commons is separate from M5Ecosystem. M5Ecosystem approval is required for restricted implementations, production systems, private member or partner services, and special commercial licenses. Public participation here does not grant that access.

Sponsorship opening today is what makes the next year of it possible.
