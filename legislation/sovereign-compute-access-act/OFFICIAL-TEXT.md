## A MODEL ACT

To guarantee free, private, unmetered access to open-weight artificial intelligence for individuals, cooperatives, and small businesses; to protect sovereign human identity and human authority over artificial intelligence agents; to preserve individual control over credentials, private computing environments, and electronically verifiable authority; and to establish state-primary, vendor-neutral standards for compute access, for other purposes.

Be it enacted by the [Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled] / [Legislature of the State of _______]:

## SEC. 1. SHORT TITLE.

This Act may be cited as the "Sovereign Compute Access Act."

## SEC. 2. FINDINGS.

Congress or the Legislature, as applicable, finds the following:

(1) Artificial intelligence compute has concentrated inside a small number of vertically integrated companies that control model weights, the cloud infrastructure on which those weights run, and, increasingly, equity stakes in one another and in the chip manufacturers that supply them.

(2) Individuals, cooperatives, and small businesses currently access artificial intelligence only on terms set unilaterally by such companies, with no guaranteed baseline of free, private, self-hosted capability.

(3) Open-weight and open-source artificial intelligence models exist at a level of capability sufficient to meet the ordinary computing needs of an individual, household, cooperative, or small business, and are already published by several of the same companies that restrict access to frontier-scale models.

(4) Consumer computing hardware already in use in households throughout the United States is, in most cases, sufficient to run such models, but is not configured to serve as validated, sovereign computing infrastructure.

(5) Current proposals for the governance of frontier artificial intelligence, including proposals published by frontier developers and legislative proposals pending before Congress, would concentrate in a small number of companies the authority to define risk, to write compliance standards, to select evaluators, and to receive protection from the antitrust laws for coordinating on such standards. A legislative response to that concentration should not itself create a new, single, centralized point of federal control.

(6) The States possess existing authority, through consumer protection law and the Uniform Commercial Code, to administer and enforce a standard of the kind established by this Act.

(7) The role of Congress under this Act is to direct existing Federal agencies to apply their existing authority to the conduct described in this Act and to authorize reciprocity among the States, consistent with the principle that a State exercising authority under this Act is not ceding that authority to a new Federal body.

(8) The public-benefit principle that a party receiving public support for research or development should make a resulting benefit available to the public, without forfeiting the party's right to commercialize its work, has operated without controversy in Federal law for more than four decades under the Patent and Trademark Law Amendments Act of 1980 (Public Law 96-517), commonly known as the Bayh-Dole Act, codified at chapter 18 of title 35, United States Code. Section 102(b) of this Act applies that same principle to publicly supported artificial intelligence compute.

(9) The increasing use of digital wallets, cryptographic identifiers, verifiable credentials, artificial intelligence agents, and electronically controlled assets makes it necessary to distinguish the continuing identity of a natural person from credentials concerning that person, authority delegated by that person, and separate assets owned or controlled by that person.

(10) A natural person's identity should not become transferable property merely because that identity is represented through cryptographic, tokenized, or other electronically verifiable technology.

(11) A persistent electronic representation of human identity may be updated over time to reflect legally recognized events, credentials, capacities, and statuses without transferring or replacing the identity of the natural person to whom that representation is permanently bound.

(12) Human authority requires that the loss, compromise, replacement, or recovery of a device, wallet, cryptographic key, or access credential not, by itself, constitute the loss or transfer of the underlying human identity.

## SEC. 3. DEFINITIONS.

In this Act:

**(1) OPEN-WEIGHT MODEL.—**The term "open-weight model" means an artificial intelligence model whose trained parameters are published for download and local execution, without regard to whether the training code or training data for the model is also published.

**(2) OPEN-SOURCE MODEL.—**The term "open-source model" means an open-weight model released under a license recognized by the Open Source Initiative, without a field-of-use restriction or monthly-active-user limitation.

**(3) SOVEREIGN POD.—**The term "Sovereign Pod" means a private, individually controlled computing and storage environment in which a Covered Tier I Holder runs a model under that holder's own authority.

**(4) COVERED TIER I HOLDER.—**The term "Covered Tier I Holder" means an individual, household, or cooperative account, or a small business, as such terms are defined by the administering registry under section 105.

**(5) COMPUTE PROVIDER.—**The term "compute provider" means a person or entity that trains, publishes, or hosts artificial intelligence models, or that manufactures or leases hardware on which such models are run.

**(6) REGISTERED SOVEREIGN NODE.—**The term "Registered Sovereign Node" means a personal computer belonging to a Covered Tier I Holder, of any chipset architecture or manufacturer, enrolled under section 104 as validated, owner-controlled computing capacity.

**(7) CONTROLLABLE ELECTRONIC RECORD.—**The term "controllable electronic record" has the meaning given that term in Article 12 of the Uniform Commercial Code, as adopted by the applicable State.

**(8) VERIFIABLE CREDENTIAL.—**The term "verifiable credential" means an electronically verifiable set of one or more claims issued or attested by an identifiable issuer concerning a natural person, legal entity, asset, authority, status, qualification, event, or other subject, the integrity and issuer of which can be electronically authenticated.

**(9) SOVEREIGN HUMAN IDENTITY TOKEN.—**The term "Sovereign Human Identity Token" means a unique, non-fungible, non-transferable, identity-bound electronic record or digital asset that represents the continuing electronically verifiable identity of one natural person and that—

(A) remains permanently associated with that natural person;

(B) is not transferable independently of that person;

(C) may be associated with verifiable credentials, attestations, permissions, authorities, and other electronic records;

(D) may undergo authenticated state-transition events without transferring or replacing the underlying human identity; and

(E) does not constitute ownership of, title to, or a property interest in the natural person represented.

**(10) STATE-TRANSITION EVENT.—**The term "state-transition event" means an authenticated update to the status, capacity, credentials, authority, or other legally relevant attributes associated with a Sovereign Human Identity Token, supported by one or more verifiable credentials, legally sufficient electronic records, lawful determinations, or other evidence from an authorized source.

**(11) ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AGENT.—**The term "artificial intelligence agent" means an artificial intelligence system or electronic agent capable of performing an action, initiating a transaction, communicating an instruction, or exercising delegated authority on behalf of a human or legal principal.

# TITLE I—SOVEREIGN COMPUTE ACCESS

## SEC. 101. UNIVERSAL FREE ACCESS MANDATE.

(a) **IN GENERAL.—**A Covered Tier I Holder is entitled to run, without charge and without a usage limitation, any open-weight or open-source model published by a compute provider, within that holder's Sovereign Pod.

(b) **PUBLICATION RECOMMENDED.—**A compute provider that releases an open-weight model is encouraged to publish the weights, any quantized variant, and the applicable license in the public registry established under section 105, concurrently and on equivalent terms for each size class the provider elects to release openly.

(c) **PROHIBITION ON CONDITIONING ACCESS.—**A compute provider may not condition access under subsection (a) on the collection of user data or telemetry, or on a requirement that inference be routed through a hosted application programming interface rather than executed locally.

## SEC. 102. OPT-IN SCALED AND FRONTIER ACCESS; COMPUTE RECIPROCITY.

(a) **SCALED AND FRONTIER ACCESS VOLUNTARY.—**

(1) **IN GENERAL.—**Access to computing capacity beyond a Covered Tier I Holder's local hardware, including pooled graphics-processing-unit compute, hosted inference, and frontier-scale models, remains a commercial service offered at the discretion of the applicable compute provider.

(2) **NO REQUIREMENT TO OPEN FRONTIER MODELS.—**Nothing in this Act requires a compute provider to release as open-weight any frontier or closed model. This Act requires only that any model voluntarily released as open-weight be made available on equivalent terms to Covered Tier I Holders under section 101.

(b) **COMPUTE RECIPROCITY FOR PUBLICLY SUPPORTED TRAINING.—**

(1) **DEFINITION.—**In this subsection, the term "publicly supported compute" means training compute obtained through a Federal or State grant, subsidized access to a government-supported national computing resource, preferential export-licensing treatment, or a direct government contract funding the training run in question. This subsection is intended to apply to artificial intelligence training compute the same public-benefit principle applied to federally funded invention under chapter 18 of title 35, United States Code (the Bayh-Dole Act), without importing that chapter's march-in or patent-specific provisions.

(2) **RECIPROCITY REQUIRED.—**A model trained using publicly supported compute in excess of a threshold established by the registry under section 105 shall have an open-weight or open-research variant of that model, or of a distilled model derived from it meeting the capability standard of section 101, released not later than 18 months after the model's first commercial or public release.

(3) **NO OBLIGATION ABSENT PUBLIC SUPPORT.—**A compute provider that trained a model without publicly supported compute is not subject to paragraph (2).

(4) **VOLUNTARY RELEASE RECOGNITION.—**The registry under section 105 shall designate a compute provider that voluntarily releases an open-weight model meeting the classification of section 101 or 102(a) as "Registry Recognized," and Federal and State agencies may treat such designation as a factor favoring that provider in procurement, independent of paragraph (2).

(5) **COMPLIANCE INCENTIVE.—**A model bearing the designation under paragraph (4) is eligible for self-attestation in place of third-party evaluation under section 301(b).

## SEC. 103. VENDOR AND CHIP NEUTRALITY.

(a) **SEPARATE MARKETS.—**A compute provider's model weights and a hardware manufacturer's chips are separate markets for purposes of this Act. A bundling arrangement, exclusivity term, or cross-equity ownership interest between a compute provider and a hardware manufacturer shall be disclosed in the registry under section 105.

(b) **MULTI-VENDOR REQUIREMENT.—**A Sovereign Pod implementation may not be restricted to the hardware of a single manufacturer. A Sovereign Pod implementation shall remain operable across not fewer than two independently manufactured hardware classes.

(c) **NATURE OF DISCLOSURE.—**Disclosure required under subsection (a) is for purposes of public transparency and does not, by itself, prohibit the disclosed investment or ownership relationship.

## SEC. 104. REGISTERED SOVEREIGN NODE PROGRAM.

(a) **PURPOSE.—**This section establishes a process by which a Covered Tier I Holder may register existing personal computing hardware, without regard to chipset architecture or manufacturer, as validated, secured capacity within a decentralized network of sovereign computing nodes.

(b) **CHIPSET NEUTRALITY.—**Eligibility for registration under this section does not depend on the manufacturer or architecture of the hardware, provided that the hardware meets the minimum classification recorded in the registry under section 105.

(c) **REFERENCE DEPLOYMENT ENVIRONMENTS.—**The registry under section 105 shall recognize free, open-source, independently auditable operating environments, not tied to a single hardware or cloud vendor, as reference implementations for registration under this section.

(d) **REGISTRATION AND VALIDATION.—**Registration of a Registered Sovereign Node shall confirm, at minimum, the owner's identity and consent, the hardware's capability classification, and a tamper-evident baseline against which the node may subsequently be checked.

(e) **LIMITATION ON ACCESS TO CONTENT.—**Validation under this section confirms hardware capability and integrity only. No party administering this Act, including a compute provider, may obtain access to a Registered Sovereign Node owner's prompts, models, files, or usage as a condition of, or result of, registration under this section.

(f) **EFFECT OF REGISTRATION.—**A validated Registered Sovereign Node is recognized as a legitimate unit of the decentralized network described in subsection (a), and its owner's entitlement under section 101 is unaffected by the chipset, manufacturer, or age of the underlying hardware.

## SEC. 105. TRANSPARENCY AND DISCLOSURE REGISTRY.

(a) **ESTABLISHMENT.—**There is established a public, versioned registry of open-weight models and Registered Sovereign Node hardware classifications, to be administered on a state-primary basis consistent with section 302.

(b) **MODEL REGISTRY CONTENTS.—**For each open-weight model, the registry shall record the publisher, version, license, total and activated parameter counts, context window, quantization, and any cross-ownership disclosed under section 103(a).

(c) **HARDWARE REGISTRY CONTENTS.—**For each Registered Sovereign Node, the registry shall record the device classification and validation status, and may not record the owner's usage or content.

(d) **SPATIAL AND WORLD-MODEL CLASSIFICATION.—**

(1) **IN GENERAL.—**Notwithstanding subsection (b), a model whose primary function is the modeling of three-dimensional space, physical dynamics, or embodied interaction (in this subsection referred to as a "spatial or world model") shall not be classified in the registry by parameter count alone.

(2) **CLASSIFICATION FACTORS.—**The registry shall classify a spatial or world model according to each of the following, to the extent known:

(A) Cumulative training compute, measured in floating-point operations.

(B) The duration for which the model maintains a consistent, revisitable representation of a modeled environment.

(C) The number and type of interactive controls the model supports.

(D) The number of sensory modalities natively processed by the model without a separate encoder.

(E) Whether the model operates under a real-time inference constraint.

(3) **REFERENCE THRESHOLD.—**For purposes of subparagraph (A), the registry may adopt as a reference point the threshold of 10^25 cumulative training floating-point operations used for general-purpose artificial intelligence models of systemic risk under the general data protection and artificial intelligence regulations of the European Union, without such adoption constituting an endorsement of any other provision of such regulations.

(4) **UPDATES.—**A threshold adopted under this subsection may be revised by the administering registry as hardware efficiency and training methods evolve.

(e) **NON-CERTIFICATION.—**An entry in the registry established under this section is informational only and does not constitute a certification of compliance, security, or fitness for use.

# TITLE II—HUMAN AGENCY, SOVEREIGN IDENTITY, AND PRIVACY

## SEC. 201. RIGHT TO SOVEREIGN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.

(a) **IN GENERAL.—**An individual may lawfully possess, download, inspect, operate, modify, and secure an open-weight artificial intelligence model on a device or infrastructure that individual controls, subject to generally applicable law governing harmful conduct.

(b) **NO CONDITIONING ON ONGOING RELATIONSHIP.—**No law enacted consistent with this Act may condition the right described in subsection (a) on a license, subscription, or continuing commercial relationship with a compute provider.

## SEC. 202. PRIVACY AND LOCAL EXECUTION.

(a) **LIMITATION ON COMPELLED ROUTING.—**A government entity may not require that a private prompt, memory, personal knowledge store, or agent activity be routed through a centralized cloud service, absent a warrant or other clearly defined statutory authority.

(b) **CONTROL OF LOCALLY GENERATED DATA.—**Data generated through local execution on a Registered Sovereign Node or a Sovereign Pod described in section 101 remains under the control of the applicable account holder, subject to otherwise applicable rights and law.

## SEC. 203. HUMAN-ROOTED AGENT AUTHORITY.

(a) **LIMITATION ON AGENT AUTHORITY.—**An artificial intelligence agent may not exercise authority in excess of that delegated to it by its human or legal principal. Each consequential action of such an agent shall carry or be capable of producing verifiable authorization, provenance, and revocation information.

(b) **REVOCATION.—**A principal may prospectively revoke authority delegated to an artificial intelligence agent at any time. Revocation takes effect as to subsequent actions after the revocation becomes effective and does not require the consent of the agent or of any compute provider.

(c) **AGENT NOT AN INDEPENDENT PRINCIPAL.—**An artificial intelligence agent may not be treated, for purposes of this Act, as an independent principal capable of acquiring authority superior to the human or legal entity that authorized it.

(d) **SEPARATION OF CREDENTIAL AND AUTHORITY.—**Possession or verification of a credential concerning a principal does not, standing alone, constitute authority for an artificial intelligence agent to act on behalf of that principal.

## SEC. 204. OPEN PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE.

(a) **AVAILABILITY OF OPEN OPTION.—**An essential citizen-facing government service shall offer an open-standard, locally deployable option consistent with title I.

(b) **PROCUREMENT.—**A government procurement process may prefer an open-weight model but may not require a private developer to publish proprietary model weights as a condition of doing business with the government.

(c) **LIMITATION ON SOLE DEPENDENCE.—**A government body may not make an essential public function permanently dependent on a single proprietary model provider without portability, continuity, escrow, and not fewer than one independently deployable open-weight alternative meeting the standard of section 101.

## SEC. 205. SOVEREIGN HUMAN IDENTITY, VERIFIABLE CREDENTIALS, AND ELECTRONIC AUTHORITY.

(a) **RIGHT TO SOVEREIGN DIGITAL IDENTITY.—**

(1) A natural person may establish, possess, and control a Sovereign Human Identity Token representing that person's continuing electronically verifiable identity.

(2) A Sovereign Human Identity Token is unique, non-fungible, non-transferable, and permanently bound to the natural person whose identity it represents.

(3) No sale, assignment, conveyance, pledge, inheritance, transfer of a cryptographic key, transfer of a wallet, or other transaction shall transfer the underlying human identity represented by the token.

(4) A Sovereign Human Identity Token does not constitute ownership of the natural person and shall not, solely because of its characterization as a token, digital asset, electronic record, or cryptographically controlled record, create a transferable property interest in that person.

(5) Nothing in this section prevents lawful ownership, transfer, encumbrance, adjudication, or other treatment of separate property, assets, accounts, or rights associated with the person.

(b) **CONTINUITY OF IDENTITY.—**

(1) The underlying human identity represented by a Sovereign Human Identity Token remains continuous notwithstanding a change in name, age, civil status, legal capacity, credential, residence, representative, appearance, presentation, or other attribute associated with the person.

(2) A lawful change to an attribute associated with a person shall be reflected, where appropriate, through a state-transition event and shall not require the creation, transfer, or replacement of the person's underlying identity.

(3) Loss, theft, compromise, destruction, or replacement of a device, wallet, private key, authentication mechanism, or access credential does not, by itself, constitute loss, termination, or transfer of the person's underlying identity.

(4) A recovery process may restore technical access or update the method by which the person exercises control without creating a new human identity or transferring the identity to the person or entity assisting in recovery.

(c) **ORIGINATING IDENTITY EVENT.—**

(1) A birth record, adoption record, naturalization record, judicial determination, or other legally sufficient identity record may serve as an originating verifiable credential or evidence supporting establishment or recognition of a Sovereign Human Identity Token.

(2) Nothing in this subsection shall be construed to characterize a birth certificate or other vital record as title to or ownership of a human being.

(3) A person whose originating records are unavailable, incomplete, corrected, sealed, foreign-issued, or otherwise outside the ordinary registration process shall not be denied recognition solely for that reason if identity may be lawfully established through other sufficient evidence.

(d) **STATE-TRANSITION EVENTS.—**

(1) A Sovereign Human Identity Token shall not change ownership during the life of the natural person.

(2) Instead, legally relevant changes may be recorded as authenticated state-transition events supported by one or more verifiable credentials, electronic records, judicial determinations, or other legally sufficient evidence issued or established by a person or authority entitled to establish the relevant fact.

(3) A state-transition event may include, as applicable—

(A) registration of birth or another originating identity event;

(B) establishment, modification, or termination of parental, custodial, or guardianship authority;

(C) attainment of legal majority or lawful emancipation;

(D) marriage, divorce, adoption, or another legally recognized change in civil status;

(E) lawful change of name;

(F) lawful correction or change of a legally recognized identity attribute;

(G) activation, modification, suspension, or termination of authority granted to a guardian, fiduciary, personal representative, dignity agent, artificial intelligence agent, or other authorized agent;

(H) a lawful determination concerning incapacity, protective custody, guardianship, disappearance, or missing-person status;

(I) restoration of personal authority or legal status following resolution or correction of a prior state;

(J) registration of death; or

(K) another legally recognized event affecting the person's status, capacity, credentials, or authority.

(4) A state-transition event changes only the applicable status, credential, capacity, authority, or attribute and does not transfer the underlying human identity.

(e) **VERIFIABLE CREDENTIALS AND ISSUER AUTHORITY.—**

(1) A verifiable credential may establish or attest to a fact, status, capacity, qualification, event, relationship, right, or authority associated with a Sovereign Human Identity Token.

(2) An issuer retains authority, subject to applicable law, over the issuance, correction, suspension, expiration, and revocation status of a credential issued by that issuer.

(3) The holder's control of a Sovereign Human Identity Token does not permit the holder to alter an issuer's authenticated attestation.

(4) An issuer's authority over a credential does not give the issuer ownership or control of the holder's Sovereign Human Identity Token or underlying human identity.

(5) Revocation, suspension, expiration, or correction of one credential does not revoke, suspend, expire, or transfer the person's underlying identity.

(f) **INDIVIDUAL CONTROL AND SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE.—**

(1) A natural person may hold, store, present, and selectively disclose credentials, attestations, and other electronic records associated with that person's identity through a digital asset wallet, personal data store, Sovereign Pod, or other private digital environment selected and controlled by that person.

(2) A verifier shall request, access, use, and retain only those attributes reasonably necessary and proportionate to a disclosed lawful purpose.

(3) Where technically practicable, a verifier shall permit verification of a necessary fact or attribute without requiring disclosure of additional identifying information unnecessary to the transaction or purpose.

(4) Credential information shall not be retained or disclosed beyond the disclosed lawful purpose except as required by law or pursuant to another lawful basis.

(g) **LEGAL EFFECT OF ELECTRONIC IDENTITY AND CREDENTIALS.—**

A Sovereign Human Identity Token, credential, authorization, presentation, signature, attestation, state-transition record, or other electronic record may not be denied legal effect solely because it—

(1) exists in electronic, tokenized, or cryptographically verifiable form;

(2) is held or presented through a digital wallet, personal data store, Sovereign Pod, decentralized system, or other private computing environment;

(3) is cryptographically signed or electronically authenticated;

(4) is associated with a decentralized or other electronically verifiable identifier; or

(5) relies upon a distributed ledger or other electronically verifiable registry.

Nothing in this subsection eliminates an otherwise applicable requirement concerning authenticity, attribution, legal capacity, form, retention, acknowledgment, notarization, eligibility, or other substantive requirement of law.

(h) **HUMAN-ROOTED DELEGATION TO ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AGENTS.—**

(1) A credential may establish identity, status, qualification, capacity, or another asserted fact, but possession or verification of a credential does not, standing alone, authorize an artificial intelligence agent to bind a human or legal principal.

(2) An artificial intelligence agent may exercise delegated authority only pursuant to authorization attributable to the human or legal principal or otherwise established by law.

(3) Such authorization shall identify, as applicable—

(A) the principal;

(B) the agent or class of agents authorized to act;

(C) the action or class of actions authorized;

(D) the person, account, property, asset, record, or subject matter to which the authority applies;

(E) applicable monetary, transactional, jurisdictional, privacy, temporal, or other material limitations;

(F) the duration or expiration of the authority; and

(G) conditions governing suspension, termination, recovery, or revocation.

(4) An artificial intelligence agent may not infer, enlarge, transfer, or redelegate authority beyond that expressly granted by the principal or otherwise provided by law.

(i) **PROTECTED IDENTITY STATE.—**

(1) Where a person is lawfully determined to be missing, incapacitated, subject to guardianship, otherwise unable to exercise control, or in another legally recognized protective condition, the Sovereign Human Identity Token may enter a protected identity state.

(2) Entry into a protected identity state does not transfer ownership of the identity token to a government agency, guardian, fiduciary, technology provider, artificial intelligence agent, or other person.

(3) A legally authorized agency, guardian, fiduciary, representative, or other person may exercise only the authority specifically granted by law, court order, authenticated delegation, or other legally sufficient instrument.

(4) Such authority shall be limited in scope and duration and shall be capable of modification or termination upon a subsequent lawful state-transition event.

(5) Where a person previously reported missing is located, a determination of incapacity is modified, or another protected status is corrected or terminated, an authenticated subsequent state-transition event may restore or update the appropriate status without creating a new identity.

(j) **DEATH AND CONTINUITY OF THE HISTORICAL IDENTITY RECORD.—**

(1) Registration of death may transition a Sovereign Human Identity Token to a terminal or historical state.

(2) The identity token of a deceased person shall not be reassigned, recycled, inherited, or transferred to another natural or legal person.

(3) A personal representative, executor, administrator, fiduciary, governmental authority, or other legally authorized person may exercise authority concerning the deceased person's estate, records, or separate assets as provided by law, but such authority does not constitute ownership of the deceased person's identity.

(4) A correction, rescission, or other lawful modification of a death record may be reflected through a subsequent authenticated state-transition event.

(k) **AUDITABLE CONTINUITY AND PRIVACY.—**

(1) A system recognizing Sovereign Human Identity Tokens under this Act shall be capable of establishing continuity between authenticated state-transition events without requiring public disclosure of the person's underlying private information.

(2) A state-transition history may establish that an authenticated change occurred without requiring disclosure of information that is sealed, confidential, expunged, protected, or otherwise restricted by law.

(3) Nothing in this section requires a person's identity history, credentials, cryptographic identifiers, or private records to be maintained on a publicly accessible blockchain or registry.

(4) Recognition of a Sovereign Human Identity Token under this Act shall not require the establishment of a centralized national identity database.

(l) **SEPARATION OF IDENTITY, CREDENTIAL, AUTHORITY, AND ASSET CONTROL.—**

For purposes of this Act, verification of one of the following shall not, by itself, establish another:

(1) the identity of the natural person;

(2) the validity of a credential concerning that person;

(3) the person's legal capacity to perform an act;

(4) the person's ownership of or authority over a particular asset or right;

(5) authority delegated by that person to an artificial intelligence agent or other representative; or

(6) legal control of a controllable electronic record or other transferable digital asset.

(m) **CONSEQUENTIAL AGENT ACTIONS.—**

An artificial intelligence agent exercising delegated authority in a transaction having material legal, financial, property, identity, employment, governmental, or other consequential effect shall generate or permit generation of a reasonably verifiable electronic record sufficient to establish—

(1) the principal on whose authority the action was taken;

(2) the agent exercising the authority;

(3) the credential, delegation, or other evidence of authority relied upon;

(4) the material scope and limitations of the authority;

(5) the date and time of the action; and

(6) the action taken and its material result.

Nothing in this subsection requires the record to be publicly accessible or to disclose private information beyond that required by applicable law.

(n) **RELATIONSHIP TO CONTROLLABLE ELECTRONIC RECORDS.—**

(1) A Sovereign Human Identity Token is not a transferable access right under this Act.

(2) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to create, solely from the technical ability to demonstrate electronic control, a right to purchase, acquire, transfer, take a security interest in, or obtain ownership of another natural person's Sovereign Human Identity Token.

(3) A person's use of a Sovereign Human Identity Token to authenticate ownership, authority, or control concerning a separate controllable electronic record does not merge the identity token with that separate asset.

(4) Where a separate electronic record constitutes a controllable electronic record under applicable commercial law, questions concerning control, transfer, priority, purchaser rights, and security interests in that separate record remain governed by applicable commercial law.

(o) **PRESERVATION OF HUMAN PERSONHOOD AND AUTHORITY.—**

No token, credential, artificial intelligence system, electronic agent, wallet provider, compute provider, software provider, issuer, verifier, registry operator, or other technological intermediary shall acquire ownership of, or superior legal authority over, a natural person solely by reason of the person's use of, representation by, or connection to such technology.

(p) **PRESERVATION OF LAW.—**

Nothing in this section shall be construed to diminish rights, duties, protections, or requirements arising under the Constitution of the United States, federal law, applicable State law, civil-rights law, privacy law, consumer-protection law, fiduciary law, family law, probate law, vital-records law, commercial law, or other applicable law.

# TITLE III—FEDERATED JURISDICTION, ANTI-CAPTURE, AND ENFORCEMENT

## SEC. 301. EVALUATOR AND STANDARDS INDEPENDENCE.

(a) **FINDING.—**Proposals under which frontier compute providers would define risk criteria, draft the applicable compliance standard, select or fund the evaluators applying that standard, and coordinate with one another under an exemption from the antitrust laws, present a structural risk of regulatory capture, independent of whether coordination among such providers has in fact occurred.

(b) **EVALUATOR DISCLOSURE.—**An entity performing a safety, capability, or compliance evaluation of a covered model shall publicly disclose its funding sources; any equity relationship with an entity it evaluates; the prior employment of its key staff at an entity it evaluates; and any material role in public advocacy concerning the standard it enforces.

(c) **LIMITATION ON GOVERNING BOARD COMPOSITION.—**An evaluator, standards body, or advisory panel administering this Act may not be governed by a board on which entities subject to evaluation hold a majority of seats. Evaluator assignments shall rotate, and evaluation methodologies and appeals shall be public.

(d) **ANTITRUST GUARDRAIL.—**A waiver permitting compute providers to coordinate on safety standards shall be narrow, public, time-limited, and independently supervised, and may not extend to coordination on price, customer allocation, the supply of open-weight competitors, or ordinary product capability.

## SEC. 302. JURISDICTION; STATE ADMINISTRATION; LIMITATION ON FEDERAL AGENCY CREATION.

(a) **STATE ADMINISTRATION.—**Each State that adopts this Act shall administer its own registry, credential-recognition process, consumer protections, and enforcement mechanism under this Act. States may recognize interoperable, open-standard systems for sovereign identity, verifiable credentials, agent authorization, electronic signatures, and controllable electronic records and may enter into interstate compacts or other lawful arrangements to provide reciprocity.

Recognition under this subsection shall distinguish identity and credential records governed by title II from transferable digital assets governed by applicable commercial law.

(b) **LIMITATION ON NEW FEDERAL AGENCY.—**Nothing in this Act shall be construed to establish, and no appropriation may be used to establish, a new Federal department or agency for the oversight of artificial intelligence. An existing Federal agency, including the Federal Trade Commission and the National Institute of Standards and Technology, shall apply its existing statutory authority to conduct described in this Act in the same manner as to any other technology.

(c) **SCOPE OF FEDERAL AUTHORITY.—**Federal authority under this Act is limited to interstate commerce disclosure requirements, export controls, antitrust review under section 301(d), and national security matters involving frontier-scale hazards, which remain subject to existing Federal national security authority and are not modified by this Act. Federal law enacted consistent with this Act does not preempt a State law that provides greater protection.

(d) **ELECTRONIC RECORDS, AGENTS, AND ATTRIBUTION.—**Questions concerning the legal effect, attribution, authentication, retention, or enforceability of electronic records, electronic signatures, and actions performed through electronic agents shall be governed by otherwise applicable Federal and State law, except to the extent expressly provided by this Act.

(e) **COMMERCIAL LAW AND ARTICLE 12.—**

(1) Article 12 of the Uniform Commercial Code, as adopted by the applicable State, shall apply to a record arising under this Act only to the extent that the record constitutes a controllable electronic record or another asset within the scope of applicable commercial law.

(2) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to classify every verifiable credential, identity record, authorization credential, agent-delegation record, or signed receipt as a controllable electronic record.

(3) Questions concerning control, transfer, priority, purchaser rights, security interests, and related commercial rights in a qualifying controllable electronic record remain governed by applicable commercial law.

(4) A Sovereign Human Identity Token described in section 205 is non-transferable under this Act and shall not acquire transferability solely because cryptographic or electronic control over its technical representation can be demonstrated.

(f) **NO OWNERSHIP OF AGENT OR HUMAN IDENTITY CREATED.—**This Act does not itself create an ownership right in an artificial intelligence agent and does not create a transferable ownership right in a natural person's identity.

## SEC. 303. PRICING AND PERFORMANCE TRANSPARENCY.

(a) **DISCLOSURE REQUIRED.—**A compute provider offering a paid service under section 102 shall disclose, at the time of a transaction, the model and version served; the base price and unit of measurement; applicable context limits and truncation behavior; rate limits and priority classes; and any model substitution, quantization, routing, caching, or degraded-service mode applied without the customer's request.

(b) **UNFAIR OR DECEPTIVE PRACTICE.—**A material, undisclosed difference between the capability advertised and the capability delivered constitutes an unfair or deceptive practice under the consumer protection law of the applicable State. This section establishes a disclosure duty and does not regulate price.

## SEC. 304. ENFORCEMENT.

(a) **DESIGNATION OF ENFORCING AUTHORITY.—**A State that adopts this Act shall designate an existing consumer protection or telecommunications regulator, rather than a newly created entity, to receive complaints and audit compliance with sections 101, 103(a), 104, 205, 301, and 303.

(b) **NATURE OF REMEDIES.—**This Act does not create a new criminal penalty. Remedies under this Act are civil and administrative, consistent with the existing consumer protection framework of the applicable State.

## SEC. 305. SEVERABILITY.

If any provision of this Act, or the application of a provision to any person or circumstance, is held invalid, the remainder of this Act, and the application of the remaining provisions to any other person or circumstance, is not affected.
