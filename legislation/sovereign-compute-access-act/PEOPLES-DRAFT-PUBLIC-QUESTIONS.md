# The People’s Draft: Questions from Everyday Life

**Foundation discussion prompts · Sovereign Compute Access Act · Public review**  
**Prepared: October 3, 2026**

You do not need to be a lawyer, engineer, or AI expert to contribute. Start with something you have experienced, something you need, or a question you have not been able to get answered.

The **People’s Draft** is our public-facing name for this proposal. Its source text is **Official Draft v1**. It has not been introduced by a legislative office or enacted into law, and it is not an adopted ICSN standard. These questions invite discussion and possible changes; they do not create rights or describe requirements already in force.

These are **Foundation-authored discussion prompts**, not independent public responses, votes, evidence of consensus, or findings about a particular provider. We welcome disagreement, counterexamples, and evidence of practices that already work well.

**Pick one question. Tell us what happened, what you need, or what you would change. Please leave out private information.**

## 1. What would you want AI to help you do?

Think about learning, work, paperwork, caregiving, managing your own belongings, or running a small business.

- What useful task would you start with?
- What would make the tool easier to understand and use?
- What should remain your decision, even when an agent does the preparation?

## 2. Who gives your agent permission—and who can take it back?

An agent might prepare a listing or invoice, but sending it, sharing information, or spending money can have different consequences.

- Which actions should always require your approval?
- Can you see what you authorized and what the agent actually did?
- Can you limit permission to one task, one recipient, or one amount?
- If you revoke permission, how should you know that future actions have stopped?
- Who should explain and help correct an action that exceeded your instructions?

**Draft connection:** Section 203 proposes limits on delegated agent authority and revocation. These prompts ask how those protections should work in practice.

## 3. What should stay private—and what should travel with you?

- What information would you prefer to keep on your own device?
- When would you choose an outside service, and what would you need to know first?
- If you changed providers, could you take your files, history, settings, and instructions with you in a usable format?
- Could you understand what would remain with the old provider and for how long?
- What recovery options would you need after a lost device, account restriction, or service closure?

**Draft connection:** Sections 201–204 address proposed local control, privacy, agent authority, and public-service continuity. A general consumer portability or recovery requirement should not be assumed from these questions; any new obligation would need explicit drafting.

## 4. Can you understand—and budget for—the AI meter?

A price per million tokens is not, by itself, an estimate for completing a task. Tokens are units used to process text; they are not necessarily whole words. Input, output, cached input, reasoning, media, and tool activity may be accounted for differently depending on the service. Subscription allowances and request-rate limits are also different from a simple price per token.

- Before starting a task, could you estimate its likely cost or a useful cost range?
- Did the provider explain what counts toward your bill or subscription allowance?
- Could you distinguish charges for your input, the answer, reused context, reasoning, tools, and other services where applicable?
- Could you see which model and service level you were receiving?
- Did the final usage record explain why the actual cost differed from the estimate?
- What examples would help you understand what a quoted unit of usage buys?
- Which cost estimates would be useful, and how should providers explain uncertainty without promising an exact result?

**Plain-language principle for discussion:** Before I start, help me understand what the work may cost. While I work, show me what I am using.

**Draft connection:** Section 303 proposes pricing and performance disclosures. Task estimates, detailed receipts, and live budget visibility are possible additions for review—not requirements expressly established by Draft v1.

## 5. What happens when the service slows down or cuts you off?

A slowdown, usage cap, context limit, and temporary capacity restriction are not necessarily the same thing. People need to understand which limit they have reached.

- Were you warned before a limit interrupted important work?
- Could you see your remaining allowance and when it would reset?
- Was the reason for the restriction explained clearly?
- If limits or service quality changed, were you told before the change affected your work?
- If you were offered an upgrade or extra usage, was the additional allowance clearly described?
- Could you save or export unfinished work and resume it later?
- What should happen if a service becomes unavailable during a task you already paid for?

**Draft connection:** Section 303 addresses rate limits, priority classes, and relevant service changes. Advance warnings, reset-time visibility, continuity options, and treatment of interrupted tasks merit explicit review.

## 6. Can you set a budget that the agent cannot exceed?

- Can you set a firm task, daily, or monthly spending limit?
- Does the limit actually stop spending, or only send a notification?
- Should the service require your approval before extra purchases or overage charges?
- Can you separately authorize a more expensive model, additional tools, or a higher service tier?
- How should the service account for retries, failed operations, or work that never completes?
- How should budget controls work for families, schools, cooperatives, and small businesses?

**Possible amendment topic:** User-controlled budgets and explicit authorization for additional charges, with clearly explained behavior when a limit is reached.

## 7. Can you meaningfully dispute an AI charge?

Tell us about your experience without posting account numbers, complete invoices, payment information, or private correspondence.

- Could you find a billing-dispute channel that actually addressed your issue?
- Could you reach a person when automated replies did not resolve it?
- Did you receive an understandable usage record and an explanation of the charge?
- Did you receive a case number and a stated response timeframe?
- Was there a way to challenge the decision or request another review?
- Were the relevant billing and usage records preserved while the dispute was open?
- What happened to your account access and the disputed amount during the review?
- What response times and review procedures would be fair and feasible for both users and providers?

**Possible amendment topics:** Accessible dispute channels; human escalation; itemized usage receipts; published response deadlines; reasoned decisions; an appeal route; relevant record preservation; and clear rules for disputed balances and account access.

These are proposals for discussion. This page does not assert that every provider lacks a dispute process or that these protections are already in Draft v1. Section 304’s proposed regulator complaint route is distinct from a provider’s own customer dispute process.

## 8. What happens if an AI output appears to contain someone else’s personal information?

A person may receive material that appears to describe another identifiable person. That deserves a clear reporting and investigation process. A matching identity alone does not establish whether the source was public information, synthetic material mixed with real details, an unauthorized disclosure, or another cause.

- Is there a clear, secure way to report the incident without spreading the information further?
- Does the provider acknowledge the report and supply an incident reference?
- If the response calls the output a “mock-up” or “synthetic,” does it explain whether that describes the document format or the information inside it?
- What investigation supports the provider’s explanation, and what findings can safely be shared with the reporter?
- How should relevant records be preserved without retaining or exposing unnecessary personal information?
- How should the provider determine whether affected people need notification or assistance under applicable requirements?
- What escalation or independent review should be available when the response does not address the concern?
- How should corrective action and follow-up be explained without exposing another person’s data or sensitive security details?

**Possible amendment topic:** A privacy-incident reporting and accountability process, including acknowledgment, proportionate investigation, protected evidence handling, reasoned explanations, and escalation.

**Please do not post the person’s name, preferences, address, private files, identifying screenshots, or the unredacted output in this public discussion.** Describe the issue at a high level and use an appropriate secure reporting channel for evidence. This question does not allege a confirmed breach by any named company.

## 9. What would make local AI practical for you?

Legal permission alone does not provide equipment, electricity, connectivity, accessible software, maintenance, or technical support.

- What would prevent you from using a model on your own computer?
- Would setup assistance, a lending program, or an accessible public workstation help?
- What role could libraries, schools, tribal institutions, or community organizations play?
- What would support need to include after installation—updates, repair, recovery, training, or troubleshooting?
- How could community access work without unnecessary identity tracking or monitoring of private activity?
- What would make a program useful for people with disabilities, limited connectivity, different languages, or limited technical experience?

**Draft connection:** Sections 101, 104, and 204, and the existing practical-access review question. Community support mechanisms remain subjects for review, not a promise that equipment or support is already funded.

## 10. What do you disagree with—or think we have missed?

- Which proposed protection would help you most?
- Which could create costs, barriers, or unintended consequences?
- Are there existing services or policies that solve part of the problem well?
- What evidence would change your view?
- Whose experience is missing from this conversation?
- What specific wording would you change?

## A simple way to respond

You can answer just one question. Use as much or as little of this structure as helps:

```text
Question or draft section:
What I experienced or need:
Who is affected:
Evidence I can safely share, or a public source:
What would have helped:
Suggested change or alternative:
```

Distinguish what you directly observed from what you suspect or infer. Criticism and disagreement are welcome. A public comment is not a vote, endorsement, official legislative submission, or guarantee that a proposed change will be adopted.
