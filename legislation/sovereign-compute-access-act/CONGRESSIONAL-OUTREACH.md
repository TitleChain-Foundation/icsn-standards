# The People’s Draft — Congressional Outreach Notice

**Foundation-reported outreach status as of October 3, 2026**

TitleChain Foundation reports that it submitted the People’s Draft of the
Sovereign Compute Access Act, together with a request for engagement, feedback,
and support toward legislative introduction, to every U.S. senator’s office
and to House member offices.

The Foundation reports notifying **441 working offices in the House complexes**.
Its reported outreach scope covers the offices of **435 voting representatives
and 6 non-voting members: 5 delegates and 1 resident commissioner**, representing
the territories and the District of Columbia as applicable.

**As of October 3, 2026, the Foundation reports that it has received no response
from any office contacted in this outreach.** The invitation remains open:
legislative offices are asked to review the draft, identify concerns and
improvements, and engage on a possible path to introduction.

## What this notice means

This notice records the Foundation’s account of its outreach, as reported by
Founder and Executive Director Pamela Norton. The office count describes the
reported outreach scope; it is not a verified recipient-by-recipient delivery
or acknowledgment record. Submission does not establish that an office received,
read, endorsed, or agreed to introduce the proposal. No response is not evidence
of any office’s position on the draft.

The People’s Draft remains **model legislation for public review**. It has not
been introduced by a legislative office or enacted into law, and it is not an
adopted ICSN standard. Sending the draft to congressional offices is distinct
from formal legislative introduction or sponsorship.

This is a dated status report, not a continuously verified count. It should be
updated when the Foundation receives a response or corrects its outreach record.

## Invitation to engage

We invite legislative offices and the public to read the proposal, ask questions,
offer evidence and specific revisions, and help develop a workable approach to
human authority, private local compute, meaningful access, and accountable
service practices.

Legislative offices interested in introduction or co-sponsorship are invited to
identify their jurisdiction and the materials they need. Legislative sponsorship
is distinct from financial or project sponsorship of the Foundation.
