# Sovereign Compute Access Act Public Review

**Draft v1 - open for public review and comment.**

This page is the public-review hub for the
[Sovereign Compute Access Act](OFFICIAL-TEXT.md). The proposal is model
legislation. It has not been enacted, introduced by a legislative office, or
reviewed as the final work product of legislative counsel. It is not an
adopted ICSN standard.

## What is open for review

Reviewers are invited to comment on:

- legal authority, constitutional issues, jurisdiction, preemption, and
  enforceability;
- the proposed use of UCC Article 12 and existing state agencies;
- privacy, civil liberties, accessibility, and human-authority protections;
- definitions of open-weight models, covered holders, providers, Sovereign
  Pods, and Registered Sovereign Nodes;
- vendor and chip neutrality, procurement, competition, and antitrust effects;
- technical feasibility, security, conformance, and implementation costs;
- factual accuracy, citations, terminology, and drafting clarity; and
- unintended consequences, alternatives, and proposed amendments.

Review comments should identify the document, section, concern, supporting
evidence, affected communities, and proposed resolution when possible.

## Public comment channels

- Use the [Draft v1 public-review Discussion](https://github.com/orgs/TitleChain-Foundation/discussions/38)
  for comments, questions, legislative-office engagement, and topics spanning
  multiple sections.
- For exact text changes, follow the repository's
  [contribution process](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) and open a focused pull request
  against the published Draft v1 files. The original publication and review
  history is preserved in [pull request 70](https://github.com/TitleChain-Foundation/icsn-standards/pull/70).
- Email [support@titlechainfoundation.org](mailto:support@titlechainfoundation.org)
  when a public thread is not appropriate. Do not send classified, privileged,
  personal, or security-sensitive material through a public channel.

GitHub comments are public-review input. They are not an official legislative
submission, endorsement, vote, or promise that a proposal will be adopted.

## Review record

Subject to maintainer capacity, the Foundation will use this lifecycle:

1. **Acknowledge and route.** Identify the relevant section and review type.
2. **Evaluate in public.** Consider evidence, legal implications, affected
   communities, technical feasibility, privacy, security, and accessibility.
3. **Record a disposition.** Mark the input proposed for acceptance, accepted
   with revision, deferred pending evidence, referred for specialized review,
   or not adopted, with a reason.
4. **Review changes.** Make substantive revisions through a pull request so the
   text and discussion remain attributable.
5. **Close with traceability.** Link the resulting commit, pull request,
   revision, or written disposition.

There is no guaranteed response time or guarantee that a proposal will be
adopted. Silence, publication, or automated validation does not constitute
approval.
