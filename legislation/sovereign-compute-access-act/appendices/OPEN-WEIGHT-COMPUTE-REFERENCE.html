The Sovereign Compute Access Act

“Americans should not be required to rent intelligence they can lawfully possess and operate for themselves.”

Reference standard for Title I (Sovereign Compute Access) of the Act. Every Personal, Cooperative, and Small Business account is entitled to free, unmetered, private access to any open-weight model a compute provider publishes — including on hardware they already own. Pooled and frontier compute stay paid and opt-in. Titles II and III (Human Agency & Privacy; Federated Jurisdiction & Anti-Capture) are covered in the full Act text.

Free · Mandatory

Tier I — Personal, Cooperative & Small Business

Bank of Me (BOM) and Bank of Us (BOU) accounts, plus small business, get unmetered access to every open-weight model on the registry, run inside their own Sovereign Pod. No telemetry requirement. No API lock-in.

Opt-in · Paid

Tiers II & III — Pooled & Frontier

Anything beyond local hardware — pooled GPUs, hosted inference, frontier-scale models — stays a commercial service, priced by each vendor. The Act doesn't force these open; it only guarantees Tier I stays free.

The full Act at a glance — three titles
Title I — Sovereign Compute Access
Sections 101–105 · detailed below
Free Tier I baseline, opt-in paid Tiers II/III, vendor and chip neutrality, the Registered Sovereign Node (BYOD) program, and the public disclosure registry.
Title II — Human Agency & Privacy
Sections 201–204
The right to possess and run open-weight AI privately; no compelled routing through centralized cloud; an AI agent can never exceed the authority its human principal delegated; essential government services must offer an open, locally deployable option.
Title III — Federated Jurisdiction & Anti-Capture
Sections 301–305
States drive enforcement via UCC Article 12 (CER); no new federal agency — existing regulators apply their existing authority; evaluator independence and antitrust guardrails against frontier-lab safety-coordination waivers.
Registry principles
  • Tiers are defined by capability, authority, and compute footprint — not by brand or a single parameter number.
  • Total parameters and activated parameters are recorded separately for mixture-of-experts models.
  • Cross-ownership between a model publisher and a chip manufacturer is disclosed, not penalized — see Hardware Neutrality below.
  • Inclusion in a tier is informative, not an endorsement or certification. Re-verify before production or legislative use.

Meta's V-JEPA 2 is a genuine open-weight world model — but at 1.2B parameters, it sits far below this tier's 500B threshold. That's not an error: JEPA-family world models use a non-autoregressive, self-supervised architecture that does not need frontier-scale parameter counts to model physical dynamics. Section 105(d) of the Act classifies spatial/world models on five axes instead of parameter count alone: cumulative training compute (FLOPs, referencing the EU AI Act's 10²⁵ threshold), temporal coherence horizon, action-space complexity, modality fusion, and real-time constraint. Parameter count is recorded as supplementary, not primary, for this category.
Anthropic: no open-weight model released to date. Claude remains closed-weight only — a real gap under this standard.

Section 104 — Bring-Your-Own-Device: Registered Sovereign Node Program

No New Purchase Required

Turn existing hardware into a validated Sovereign Node

Tens of millions of functional computers already sit idle or under-used in American homes. Any Covered Tier I holder can register their own PC or Mac — any chipset, any manufacturer — as a Registered Sovereign Node, part of a decentralized Internet of Sovereign Things.

Owner Control, Not Surveillance

Registration verifies capability, not content

Validation confirms hardware capability and a tamper-evident baseline. It grants no party — not TitleChain Foundation, not ICSN, not any compute provider — access to the owner's prompts, models, files, or usage. The Node stays a private M5POD.

Asahi Linux
Apple Silicon · M1–M4 class
Free, open-source native Linux port for Apple Silicon Macs. Converts an existing Mac to a fully open Sovereign Node without proprietary OS dependency.
Omarchy
x86 · Intel or AMD
Free, MIT-licensed, opinionated Arch Linux/Hyprland deployment. Standardized, reproducible base environment for a Sovereign Node on any x86 machine.
Registration path
M5-H0→H5 hardware classification
Same classification process used elsewhere in the standard, producing a device-identity and capability record consistent with ICSN Hardware Onboarding Standard RFC 0001.
Eligibility never depends on chipset or brand — an Apple, Intel, or AMD machine qualifies equally once it clears the minimum hardware classification. This is the mechanism that makes Tier I access real for households that already own a computer rather than a purchase requirement in disguise.
Section 102(b) — Compute Reciprocity: the incentive to open up more models

If public compute was used

Reciprocity is required, not optional

A model trained using Publicly Supported Compute — a government grant, a subsidized national compute resource, preferential export treatment, or direct government contract — must have an open-weight variant released within 18 months. Modeled on the Bayh-Dole Act (35 U.S.C. Ch. 18): public benefit as the price of public support, without banning private commercialization.

If a company opens up voluntarily

Compliance gets cheaper, not just favored

A voluntary open-weight release earns "Registry Recognized" status: eligible for procurement consideration under Section 102(b)(4), and eligible for self-attestation in place of third-party evaluation under Section 102(b)(5) — a direct cost saving, not just a badge.

No provider is compelled to open a model built entirely on private compute (Section 102(a)(2)). This section applies where public support contributed to the training run and recognizes voluntary open releases.

Why mandate Tier I specifically

Every major model publisher already releases something open — OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, and Alibaba all ship open-weight models today. The Act doesn't ask them to open anything new; it asks that what they already publish be guaranteed, free, to every Personal, Cooperative, and Small Business account, not offered at a vendor's discretion.

Sovereignty by design

Every tier runs inside a private M5POD under the holder's own authority. What downloads into that pod is chosen by the holder. Paid tiers remain available for anyone who wants more — but no one is required to pay for a baseline that already exists in the open.
