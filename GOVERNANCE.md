# ICSN Standards Governance

> Working governance policy for this repository. It governs the open standards process and is not a substitute for the TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust instrument, the TitleChain Foundation's legal governing documents, board resolutions, or applicable law.

## Constitutional floor

The repository process operates within a broader constitutional stewardship model described in [TRUST-AND-NON-CAPTURE.md](TRUST-AND-NON-CAPTURE.md).

A GitHub issue, RFC, vote, sponsor agreement, implementation decision, or repository permission cannot by itself amend the TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust, create beneficiary rights in the Trust, transfer Trust Property, or grant a sponsor, vendor, implementation, founder, or contributor unilateral authority over the protected purpose.

The standards process may debate and change technical designs, implementation guidance, conformance requirements, and repository policy. It may not represent that a repository change overrides a controlling legal instrument or applicable law.

The constitutional direction includes human-rooted authority, privacy, meaningful consent, portability, the right to exit, implementation neutrality, and resistance to capture through funding, dependency, surveillance, or control.

## Purpose

The TitleChain Foundation stewards open specifications for human-rooted identity, sovereign networks, interoperability and conformance. The process is public, evidence-led and designed to include implementers and affected communities.

## Roles

- **Contributors** propose ideas, evidence, issues and pull requests.
- **Editors** maintain specification text and help proposals meet repository requirements.
- **Maintainers** manage repository operations, moderation and releases.
- **Standards stewards** decide whether an RFC advances in maturity after public review.

Role assignment is based on sustained contribution, sound judgment, respectful participation and disclosed conflicts of interest. Repository permissions do not by themselves confer legal authority to speak for the Foundation.

## Decision process

1. Material proposals begin as a public Discussion.
2. A documented RFC defines the problem, affected parties, design, alternatives, risks and conformance implications.
3. Editors check completeness and open the proposal for review.
4. Stewards seek reasoned consensus. If consensus is not achievable, the decision and material objections are recorded.
5. Changes advance through Draft, Candidate, Stable and Deprecated stages.

Stable status requires implementation evidence, security and privacy review, conformance criteria, public review, and an affirmative recorded decision by authorized standards stewards.

## Openness and accountability

Meeting notes, decisions, RFC history and declared conflicts should be public unless confidentiality is required for security, privacy, legal duties or protected community knowledge.

Public openness must not be confused with publication of private identity records, credentials, keys, member data, confidential business information, protected cultural knowledge, or security-sensitive material.

## Independence

M5 products may implement and help validate these standards, but no single vendor or implementation defines the standard. Conformance claims must be based on published criteria.

Sponsorship, grants, partnerships, cohort support, marketplace recognition and other financial support do not purchase standards authority, votes, conformance status, adoption, preferential review or guaranteed acceptance of a proposal.

Editors and stewards must disclose material conflicts and recuse themselves when they cannot act impartially. Funding may support a public workstream or milestone, but the resulting work remains subject to the ordinary review and maturity process.

No sponsor relationship grants access to private member data, ownership of an IAM identity, or a requirement that participants adopt the sponsor's platform, cloud, model, wallet, bank, or service.

## Public infrastructure and optional activation

ICSN standards participation does not require an IAM account or M5 activation.

The Foundation may maintain public-interest activation infrastructure and sponsored programs, including the no-charge foundational IAM pathway described in [PUBLIC-INFRASTRUCTURE-COMMITMENT.md](PUBLIC-INFRASTRUCTURE-COMMITMENT.md). Those programs are separate from standards authority and must preserve informed consent, privacy, portability, and the right to decline.

## Amendments

This repository policy may be amended through the same RFC and public-review process. Material governance changes should receive longer review than routine editorial changes.

An amendment to this file does not purport to amend the TitleChain Sovereign Purpose Trust or another controlling legal instrument.
